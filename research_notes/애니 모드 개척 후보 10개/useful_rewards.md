# Useful Non-Cosmetic, Non-Multiplier Rewards ("Capacity / Option / Access") for an Endless Eyes-Free Anime Mode

Source note: every direct page fetch failed in this session. Bulbapedia, wiki.factorio.com and stardewvalleywiki.com did not resolve because they are outside the session's allowed network domains. So **all findings below come from search-result snippets and summaries** (marked [snippet]), not from full pages I read. Wiki numbers also change between game versions, and several sources disagreed, as noted inline. Anything not backed by a snippet is under Inferences or Gaps.

## What categories of "expand what you can do" rewards exist, and what are concrete examples?

### Takeaway
Real games use at least nine reward families that add capacity, options or access rather than output: (1) land/space, (2) storage and inventory capacity, (3) building/worker/base slots, (4) creature housing and box capacity, (5) map access and routes, (6) recipes/blueprints and alternate recipes, (7) visitor/spawn variety, (8) rare-encounter odds, and (9) mobility/QoL/infrastructure. The best fits are the ones that are **finite per unit but can be extended endlessly by adding new units** (another plot, another region, another recipe). A single number that grows forever is a poor fit.

### Cited Findings

**1. Land / space (the user's preferred direction)**
- Slime Rancher's ranch is split into purchasable expansions. The wiki counts 26 plots in total: 8 in the main area, 4 in the Overgrowth, 5 in the Grotto, 4 in the Docks and 5 in the Lab. The Overgrowth reportedly costs 1,795 Newbucks and the Lab 10,000 Newbucks. Until you buy the Lab expansion, a force field blocks the path to it [snippet] — [Slime Rancher Wiki: The Ranch](https://slimerancher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Ranch); [BlueStacks map guide](https://www.bluestacks.com/blog/game-guides/slime-rancher/src-map-guide-en.html); [Overgrowth wiki](https://slimerancher.fandom.com/wiki/Overgrowth). Sources disagree on the Docks' cost and route.
- Buying the Lab expansion also unlocks Slime Science, so in Slime Rancher a land unlock doubles as a feature unlock [snippet] — [HyperX blog](https://blog.hyperx.com/article/2974/slime-rancher-how-to-get-the-slime-science-lab-and-craft-gadgets).
- Animal Crossing: New Horizons gates terraforming (Island Designer) behind late-game progress, reportedly a 3-star island rating or a Tom Nook task chain. Cliff construction and waterscaping permits are reportedly 6,000 each, but sources disagree on whether that is Bells or Nook Miles. Bridges and inclines let the player and villagers reach new areas [snippet] — [GameWith](https://gamewith.net/animal-crossing-new-horizons/article/show/15583); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/animal-crossing-new-horizons-island-designer-app); [Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/117102/how-to-build-bridges-inclines-animal-crossing-new-horizons).

**2. Storage / inventory capacity**
- Satisfactory: of the Hard Drives found at crash sites, two unlock inventory expansions instead of alternate recipes. "Inflated Pocket Dimension" adds 6 inventory slots and "Expanded Toolbelt" adds 1 hand slot. A Dimensional Depot upgrade is listed as "store 2 stacks per item type" [snippet] — [Satisfactory wiki.gg: Hard Drive](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Hard_Drive); [Research](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Research).
- Dyson Sphere Program: the mecha "Inventory Capacity" upgrade line has 6 tiers, from 50 slots up to 120. The level-6 entry is "+2 rows, 120 inventory capacity, 24 logistics slots". The "Mechanical Frame" tiers raise walk speed up to 16 m/s, and the Communication Control line adds construction drones (+1 per tier) [snippet] — [DSP Wiki: Upgrades](https://www.dsp-wiki.com/Upgrades); [Inventory Capacity](https://dev.dsp-wiki.com/Inventory_Capacity_(Upgrade)).

**3. Building / worker / base slots**
- Palworld: base level gates both how many Pals can work at a base and how many bases you can have. One 1.0 table shows 11 working Pals at base level 11 and 12 at level 12. Newer sources say the maximum base level is 25 and the base count went from 3 to 4 at level 25. Older guides cite 3 bases and a 15-Pal cap. World settings sliders can override these caps [snippet] — [Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/?p=452997); [Palworld wiki.gg: Bases](https://palworld.wiki.gg/wiki/Bases); [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/newsroom/palworld-max-number-of-pals-working-at-base-explained/z568cd). Sources conflict, probably because of version differences.

**4. Creature housing / box capacity**
- Pokémon Sleep: a 2025 update raised Pokémon Box capacity from 1,200 to 1,400. It also raised the research rank, helper Pokémon level and recipe level caps from 65 to 70. Caps have been raised step by step since launch (55→60→65→70) [snippet] — [PokémonGOHub](https://pokemongohub.net/post/sleep/pokemon-sleep-major-update-level-cap-increase-and-more/); [Game8](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Sleep/archives/446009).
- Pokémon Sleep: cooking pot size grows through Sleep Style Dex collection goals. The first expansion comes at 35 Sleep Styles, each expansion adds +3, and one guide lists a maximum of 33 at 110 styles [snippet] — [Serebii: Sleep Style Dex Goals](https://serebii.net/pokemonsleep/dexgoals.shtml). Here, collection breadth (not output) unlocks capacity.

**5. Map access / routes**
- Stardew Valley Community Center: the Crafts Room repairs the bridge east of the Mines (opening the Quarry), the Pantry rebuilds the greenhouse, the Boiler Room repairs the minecarts (fast travel between the Bus Stop, Quarry, Mines and Blacksmith), and the Vault repairs the bus to Calico Desert [snippet] — [Stardew Valley Wiki: Bundles](https://www.stardewvalleywiki.com/bundles); [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/stardew-valley-community-center-guide/). Some guides mislabel rooms, and gold costs for the Joja-route repairs differ between game versions.
- Slime Rancher: the Docks are reached through a teleporter or ramp from the Overgrowth, so buying land also opens new routes [snippet] — [Overgrowth wiki](https://slimerancher.fandom.com/wiki/Overgrowth); [Almar's Guides](https://almarsguides.com/Computer/Games/SlimeRancher/Walkthrough/Part1/).

**6. Recipes / blueprints / alternate recipes**
- Satisfactory: each Hard Drive offers a choice between two randomly chosen alternate recipes, with one reroll. Drives can be stored in a library so the choice can wait, and stored rewards stop repeating. The totals listed are 118 crash sites, 106 alternate recipes, 2 inventory expansions and 111 total drives used. The wiki says "alternate recipes cannot be considered upgrades", meaning they are sidegrades or options [snippet] — [Satisfactory wiki.gg: Alternate Recipes](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Alternate_Recipes); [Hard Drive](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Hard_Drive).

**7. Visitor / wild creature spawns (variety, not yield)**
- Pokémon Sleep: Drowsy Power = Snorlax Strength × Sleep Score. Higher Drowsy Power brings more visiting Pokémon and rarer ones. Snorlax rank (Basic 1 to Master 20) unlocks more species and Sleep Styles per research area. Each Snorlax lasts one week and then resets (sources disagree on whether the reset day is Sunday or Monday) [snippet] — [Game8](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Sleep/archives/418690); [Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/pokemon/ways-increase-snorlax-drowsy-power-pokemon-sleep); [Serebii: Snorlax Ratings](https://serebii.net/pokemonsleep/snorlaxratings.shtml).
- Pikmin Bloom: planting enough flowers near a landmark's big flower makes it bloom and grants an expedition. Expeditions run on real time and location [snippet] — [Pikmin Wiki](https://www.pikminwiki.com/Pb); [pikminbloom.com](https://pikminbloom.com/gameplay).

**8. Rare-encounter odds (hunting meters)**
- Pokémon Legends: Arceus: reaching Research Level 10 on a species roughly doubles its shiny odds (base 1/4,096 to about 1/2,048). Perfect research gives about 1/1,024. Reaching Level 10 on all 242 species unlocks the Shiny Charm (about 1/819 at Level 10; about 1/585 with perfect research plus the charm). Mass outbreaks stack to about 1/152 down to 1/128. You do not need to finish every task to reach Level 10 [snippet] — [Serebii](https://serebii.net/legendsarceus/shinypokemon.shtml); [PokémonGOHub](https://pokemongohub.net/post/guide/pokemon-legends-arceus-shiny-rates/); [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/guides/pokemon-legends-arceus-shiny-pokemon-base-odds-and-how-to-get-shiny-pokemon).
- Pokémon Scarlet/Violet: clearing 30 Pokémon in a Mass Outbreak adds one shiny roll (about 1/2,048), and 60 or more adds two (about 1/1,366). Each outbreak holds about 120 Pokémon before it disappears [snippet] — [Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/pokemon/news/what-are-the-shiny-odds-in-pokemon-scarlet-and-violet); [Serebii: Mass Outbreaks](https://SEREBII.NET/scarletviolet/massoutbreaks.shtml).
- Pokémon Let's Go: the Catch Combo grows as you catch the same species in a row, and catching an evolution breaks it. Pokémon.com notes that above a 30 combo, individual stat gains stop improving [snippet] — [Pokémon.com](https://www.pokemon.com/uk/strategy/keep-that-catch-combo-going); [Serebii: Catch Combo](https://serebii.net/letsgopikachueevee/catchcombo.shtml).
- Monster Hunter: World: research points come from tracks, part breaks, traps and turf wars. A monster's research level rises only when you visit the Chief Ecologist. Collecting enough tracks of a tempered monster unlocks an investigation for it, and the threat tier is gated by Hunter Rank (tier 1 from HR 13, tier 2 from HR 30, tier 3 from HR 50) [snippet] — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/103130/monster-hunter-world---what-are-research-points); [GameSkinny](https://www.gameskinny.com/drxo1/monster-hunter-world-tempered-monster-investigations-guide). Some guides are flagged as outdated.

**9. Mobility / QoL / infrastructure**
- Stardew minecarts (fast travel) and the DSP mecha speed and drone tiers are listed above. In both, the reward is reach and convenience, not production [snippet] — [Stardew Valley Wiki: Bundles](https://www.stardewvalleywiki.com/bundles); [DSP Wiki: Upgrades](https://www.dsp-wiki.com/Upgrades).

### Inferences
- Rewards from **finite, enumerable pools** fit best: a list of plots, regions, recipes or species where each item is unlocked once. Endlessness then comes from authoring or procedurally generating more items (new frontier tiles, new regions), not from raising one number forever. Satisfactory's "stored rewards stop repeating" rule is the model for this.
- Families 1, 4, 5 and 7 are the cleanest fit for a monster farm + Create-style factory. Land expands where you can build, but buildings still have to be placed, powered and supplied. Housing capacity limits how many monsters you keep, not how much each produces. Routes and regions give access to new resources and species. Visitor variety affects what you can collect, not output rates.
- Family 3 (worker slots, as in Palworld) and family 2 (storage) sit near the line. More worker slots can indirectly raise throughput, and storage is neutral to throughput but changes buffering. Keep them per-site and hard-capped if used.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm current Satisfactory, Palworld or Animal Crossing values from primary pages, because fetches were blocked and sources disagree by version.
- I found no sourced details on Shapez, Create mod progression, Pokémon GO step-based features beyond Adventure Sync, or Ring Fit in this pass.
- I found no source on visitor/customer/trade-partner unlocks (for example, new delivery contracts in farming sims).

## How do endless versions of these rewards stay balanced (soft caps, diminishing returns, rotation, prestige)?

### Takeaway
The pattern that recurs across these games: **(a) exponential or steep cost curves on infinite tracks, (b) caps raised by patch (not by grinding), (c) weekly resets of the progress meter that keep the collection permanent, and (d) caps on how much passive activity counts per day.** Every one of these is a way to make endless effort yield bounded, stepwise gains.

### Cited Findings
- **Exponential infinite research (Factorio):** most infinite techs follow a geometric cost series. Worker robot speed costs 1,000 × 2^(N−F−1) research units in the base game, for +65% per level. Mining productivity is an exception with linear cost, 2,500 × (N−F) in the base game and 1,000 × (N−F) in Space Age. The wiki's rule of thumb: the cumulative cost to reach level N is about the cost of level N+1 alone. A mod author still argues that infinite costs "scale too fast" [snippet] — [Factorio Wiki: Infinite research breakpoints](https://wiki.factorio.com/Infinite_research_breakpoints); [Infinite Research Cost Tweaker](https://mods.factorio.com/mod/InfiniteResearchCostTweaker).
- **Caps raised by the developer, not by grinding (Pokémon Sleep):** the research rank and Pokémon level caps moved 55→60→65→70 across updates, and box capacity moved 1,200→1,400 [snippet] — [PokémonGOHub](https://pokemongohub.net/post/sleep/pokemon-sleep-major-update-level-cap-increase-and-more/).
- **Weekly reset with a permanent collection (Pokémon Sleep):** Snorlax Strength resets each week, while the Pokémon, Sleep Style Dex and pot expansions you earned are kept [snippet] — [Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/pokemon/ways-increase-snorlax-drowsy-power-pokemon-sleep); [Serebii: Dex Goals](https://serebii.net/pokemonsleep/dexgoals.shtml).
- **Collection-gated capacity (Pokémon Sleep pot):** pot expansions come from the number of distinct Sleep Styles collected, in fixed +3 steps up to a hard maximum [snippet] — [Serebii: Dex Goals](https://serebii.net/pokemonsleep/dexgoals.shtml).
- **Thresholds that stop paying off (shiny odds):** the Legends: Arceus odds improve only at discrete steps (Research Level 10, perfect research, the charm). Scarlet/Violet outbreaks improve only at 30 and 60 clears and do nothing beyond 60. Let's Go combo stat benefits plateau above 30 [snippet] — [Serebii LA](https://serebii.net/legendsarceus/shinypokemon.shtml); [Dot Esports SV](https://dotesports.com/pokemon/news/what-are-the-shiny-odds-in-pokemon-scarlet-and-violet); [Pokémon.com](https://www.pokemon.com/uk/strategy/keep-that-catch-combo-going).
- **Offline/passive caps (idle games):** Egg, Inc. limits offline progress to 2 hours. A Game Developer article argues the purpose is to push players toward active play and reduce time's influence on progression. Clicker Heroes has no offline cap, and other idle frameworks use 24 hours [snippet] — [Game Developer: Math of Idle Games III](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-math-of-idle-games-part-iii); [Clicker Heroes blog](https://blog.clickerheroes.com/offline-progression-in-clicker-heroes/embed/).
- **Weekly tiered milestones (Pokémon GO Adventure Sync):** rewards come at 5, 25 and 50 km per week (a 100 km tier was added later) and are claimed on Monday. Reported reward contents differ between sources and versions [snippet] — [Serebii: Adventure Sync](https://serebii.net/pokemongo/adventuresync.shtml); [Comicbook](https://comicbook.com/gaming/news/pokemon-go-adventure-sync-100-km).

### Inferences
- For the anime mode: a weekly or daily meter that converts hours into a **fixed, small set of unlocks**, such as one land tile, one region step or one housing slot, with extra hours beyond the cap feeding a slower overflow track. This mirrors Sleep's 8.5-hour cap plus weekly reset, and GO's weekly tiers.
- Use a geometric cost per *repeat* (the Factorio approach) only if a repeating reward is unavoidable. Prefer **new content items** over repeatable stat levels.

### Gaps
- No sourced information on prestige systems specifically tied to capacity or land.
- Universe Matrix and infinite upgrades in Dyson Sphere Program were not confirmed.

## Which games convert passive / real-world / background activity into real progress, and how do they avoid making it mandatory or overpowered, and present results ("check when you want")?

### Takeaway
Pokémon Sleep, Pokémon GO Adventure Sync, Pikmin Bloom and Forest all turn a background activity into **access and collection** rewards (visitors, species, eggs, expeditions, new tree types), not into core power. They keep it in bounds with **daily caps on counted time, weekly milestone tiers, real-time-gated expeditions, and a morning or weekly summary screen** instead of live check-ins.

### Cited Findings
- **Pokémon Sleep caps counted sleep:** only the first 8 h 30 min of recognized sleep counts toward the maximum score of 100. Two sessions a day share that 100 limit, and a session must be at least 90 minutes. Short sessions still add to Drowsy Power and encounters (a 90-minute session scores at most about 20) [snippet] — [Game8](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Sleep/archives/418707); [unanswered.io](https://unanswered.io/guide/how-to-get-100-sleep-score-pokemon-sleep); [Serebii: Sleep Tracking](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonsleep/sleeptracking.shtml).
- **Pokémon Sleep avoids rewarding specific schedules:** developers said they "intentionally did not design the app around rewarding specific sleep schedules, such as 'early bird' or 'night owl'", because that might encourage users to stay up late or cut sleep short to catch certain Pokémon [snippet] — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/20230726-20283/); [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/pokemon-sleep-took-so-long-to-come-out-because-you-cant-playtest-sleep).
- Counterpoint: Nintendo Life's review said the app can't account for irregular sleep and can feel judgmental about bad sleep habits [snippet] — [Nintendo Life review](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/mobile/pokemon-sleep).
- **Pokémon Sleep presents results the next morning:** Drowsy Power at wake time decides which visitors appear. The weekly Snorlax rank keeps the week's progress visible [snippet] — [Game8](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Sleep/archives/418690).
- **Pokémon GO Adventure Sync:** counts walking while the app is closed through Apple Health or Google Fit, and turns it into Buddy Candy and egg hatching. Rewards come in weekly milestone tiers claimed on Monday, and there is a weekly summary of incubator and candy progress. It requires Trainer Level 5 or above, and players have complained about inaccurate distance tracking [snippet] — [Pokémon GO: Adventure Sync](https://pokemongo.com/en/news/adventure-sync); [Niantic Help](https://niantic.helpshift.com/hc/faq/3265-adventure-sync); [Comicbook](https://comicbook.com/gaming/news/pokemon-go-adventure-sync-100-km).
- **Pikmin Bloom:** walking plants flowers using petals as fuel. Enough flowers near a landmark unlock an expedition that runs on real time and location. Event step challenges escalate in tiers and reward Gold Seedlings (mostly cosmetic Decor Pikmin) [snippet] — [Pikmin Wiki](https://www.pikminwiki.com/Pb); [Nintendo news](https://www.nintendo.com/whatsnew/mobilenews-step-up-to-earn-pikmin-3-deluxe-items-in-pikmin-bloom/).
- **Forest:** a focus session grows a tree, and leaving the app early kills it. Coins buy new tree species (reportedly over 1,000 coins each) and can be redeemed for real trees through Trees for the Future. One listing says real-tree planting is limited to 5 per user for budget reasons. The paid tier gives 3× coins [snippet] — [App Store: Forest](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id866450515).
- **Habitica:** completed real-world tasks damage party quest bosses, and quests drop equipment and pet eggs. Pets grow into mounts by feeding. Missed Dailies cost HP, so there is a penalty mechanic [snippet] — [Habitica Wiki](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Habitica_Wiki); [Internet Matters](https://www.internetmatters.org/advice/apps-and-platforms/wellbeing/habitica/).

### Inferences
- The shared shape is: **background activity → a capped "charge" meter → converted at a session boundary into new things you can collect or reach** (visitors, species, eggs, expeditions, new tree types). It is almost never converted into raw output multipliers. This matches the user's constraints.
- Forest's and Habitica's punishment for quitting (a withered tree, lost HP) conflicts with the "no fail" requirement. Pokémon Sleep and Adventure Sync, which reward and never penalize, are the right models.
- Present results once, at the end of the session, as a summary like Sleep's morning screen or GO's weekly summary. For example: "while you watched, your crew cleared 3 frontier tiles and a wandering monster settled in." Do not push mid-session alerts. Vibration can mark meter milestones without needing the screen.
- Keep it optional with a daily cap (Sleep's 8.5 h) and some return on short sessions (Sleep's 90-minute naps still count), so the mode adds value without becoming a second job.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced material on Focus Plant, or on how Pokémon Sleep handles players who skip days. It appears there is no penalty, but this is not confirmed.
- I found no sourced data on whether these designs measurably reduce feelings of obligation (no player-psychology studies were retrieved).

## Which of these can be made explicitly non-automatable in a factory game without disrupting factory throughput math?

### Takeaway
Rewards that change **where, what and with whom** you can build, rather than **how fast** things run, leave the throughput math intact: land tiles, region and route access, housing slots, species and visitor variety, rare-encounter odds, and one-time recipe or blueprint options. They can be made non-automatable by tying them to a player-bound meter (like Sleep's per-account score) instead of to an item that machines could produce.

### Cited Findings
- Satisfactory treats alternate recipes as sidegrades ("cannot be considered upgrades"), and inventory expansions are one-time and finite. These are options that change design choices, not built-in rate boosts [snippet] — [Satisfactory wiki.gg](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Alternate_Recipes).
- Factorio's infinite techs (robot speed, mining productivity) do change throughput directly. They are an example of what to avoid under the user's constraints [snippet] — [Factorio Wiki](https://wiki.factorio.com/Infinite_research_breakpoints).
- Pokémon Sleep's meter is fed only by the player's tracked sleep, which no in-game action can produce, so it cannot be automated by design [snippet] — [Game8](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Sleep/archives/418690).
- In Monster Hunter: World, research levels advance only through a manual visit to the Chief Ecologist. This is a human-in-the-loop gate on a passively accumulated resource [snippet] — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/103130/monster-hunter-world---what-are-research-points).

### Inferences (design candidates for the monster-farm + Create-style factory)
These are ranked by how well each fits the user's constraints. They are not sourced and are my synthesis from the findings above.
1. **Frontier clearing (land tiles):** the anime-mode meter clears one tile or chunk of blocked land per threshold, like the Slime Rancher and Animal Crossing expansions. It is endless because the frontier can be procedural. The factory math is untouched, since new land still needs machines, power and supply. Use a rising per-tile cost (Factorio-style geometric or linear curve) or fixed-cost tiles that get further from the base.
2. **Route and road building to new regions:** like the Stardew bridge, minecarts and bus. The meter builds one bridge, tunnel or road segment, and each completed route opens a region with new raw resources and wild monster species. Regions are finite but can be added in later content.
3. **Monster housing / ranch pen capacity:** like the Pokémon Sleep box (1,200→1,400) and Palworld's per-base Pal cap. It is hard-capped per pen, and adding new pens needs land, which ties it to #1. It is neutral to throughput as long as workers aren't tied to output; if monsters work machines, cap work slots separately.
4. **Wild visitor and spawn variety:** like Pokémon Sleep's Drowsy Power → visitors. Hours of anime mode fill a "lure" meter, and at the end of the session new wild species, or rarer ones, appear on the cleared land for the player to tame. This affects collection, not production, and a weekly reset plus permanent dex keeps it bounded.
5. **Rare-encounter odds with discrete thresholds:** like the Legends: Arceus Research Level 10 and SV outbreak 30/60 steps. It only applies to encounters and never to machine output. Use steps that plateau rather than linear growth.
6. **Survey and research of blueprints/alternate recipes:** like the Satisfactory hard drives. The meter "decodes" one alternate recipe or new machine option per threshold, from a finite pool that never repeats. The user should confirm, because alternates can shift ratios (though they are designed as sidegrades).
7. **Fast-travel or logistics anchors:** like the Stardew minecarts. They help the player get around but do not move items. If they moved items, they would change throughput, so keep them player-only.
- **Non-automatability:** make the meter account-bound and fed only by anime-mode input (the fixed button sequence or stick rotation), with no item form a machine could produce. Add a daily cap like Sleep's 8.5 h on counted hours so no one is pushed to run it around the clock (an inference from the Sleep and Egg, Inc. patterns).
- **Hard to automate even with a controller macro:** a macro could replay the button sequence, but the daily cap limits how much that gains. A stronger guard against macros is not addressed by any source found.

### Gaps
- No source discusses anti-macro or anti-turbo-controller measures for input-based passive modes.
- No source directly analyzes the effect of land expansion or housing caps on throughput in Create-mod or Shapez-style games. The "neutral to throughput" claim is an inference.
- No sourced examples of "new trade partners / delivery contracts" or "faction reputation" as non-multiplier unlocks were found in this pass.
