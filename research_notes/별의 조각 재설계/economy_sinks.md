Design context, restated so this file stands alone: 별의 조각 (star fragments) are earned only in the eyes-free anime mode of a monster-farm plus Create-style factory game. They must be genuinely needed, and a stockpile should feel reassuring (든든). They must not change factory production or ratio math, and must not tie factory size to anime watch time. They must stay useful forever, with no inflation, no endless content authoring and no failure or penalty systems. Already rejected: convenience-only uses, cosmetics, gacha, monster lures/gifts/storage, land-expansion merge, time-skips, wish events, mega projects, retry insurance.

## 1. Faucet/sink theory: which patterns make a sink endless without new content?

### Takeaway
The classic MMO lesson, from Ultima Online through WoW, is that fixed-price, content-bound sinks (one-off purchases, vendor items, mounts) stop working as wealth grows. Sinks that last are of two kinds. Some are **intrinsic**: spending the currency *is* the useful act, as with PoE orbs. Others are **state-dependent**: cost scales with what the player has or does, which is Dormans' *dynamic friction*, or escalates per use, which is the *stopping mechanism*. Both stay valuable with no new content, because demand is generated by the player's own growing system.

### Cited Findings
- **Origin of the faucet/drain model.** Zach Simpson's 1999 paper "The In-Game Economics of Ultima Online" is credited with popularizing the faucet-drain framing. Raph Koster's later SWG stats post describes faucet money as "minted… created by the game system". [snippet] — [Simpson 1999 (gwern mirror)](https://gwern.net/doc/economics/mechanism-design/1999-04-07-simpson-theingameconomicsofultimaonline.html); [Koster, Astromech Stats](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=25954)
- **UO had too few drains even with item decay.** Koster writes that hoards broke up mainly because players quit, houses decayed and left their contents to rot, or rich players split their stashes among friends. So the main de facto drain was attrition (loss), not a designed sink. [snippet] — [Koster, The evolution of UO's economy](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=16263)
- **Hoarding counts as a drain.** A summary of drain types says hoarding does not destroy money but takes it out of circulation, so it acts like a drain. [snippet] — [Koster, economy page](https://raphkoster.com/gaming/economy.shtml) (attribution within the result set is uncertain)
- **Housing upkeep and decay.** An Engadget housing retrospective argues that maintenance and taxes can drain inflated currency from rich landholders, and calls harsh decay and land caps "unpleasant but absolutely mandatory for open world housing". This is the penalty-style sink the user rules out. [snippet] — [Engadget, Lessons from the history of MMO housing](https://www.engadget.com/2014/09/19/working-as-intended-lessons-from-the-history-of-mmo-housing/)
- **Castronova.** He studied EverQuest's Norrath as a real economy (production, labor, inflation, exchange rates; the currency traded at USD 0.0107). A secondary review of *Synthetic Worlds* says MMOs are inherently prone to inflation, with taxes and sinks as the counter. [snippet] — [Castronova, Game Studies 3(2)](https://gamestudies.org/0302/castronova/); [CESifo WP618](https://www.cesifo.org/DocDL/cesifo_wp618.pdf)
- **EVE Online** publishes Monthly Economic Reports. They have broken out ISK faucets and sinks by category since at least 2016, and since February 2021 they ship a 3-year CSV. In the March 2026 MER total faucets rose 17%. In May 2026 they rose 26%, driven by bounty prizes and commodities. A player thread notes that mission items act as either faucet or sink depending on whether they are sold or consumed. [snippet] — [MER March 2026](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/monthly-economic-report-march-2026); [MER May 2026](https://www.eveonline.com/es/news/view/monthly-economic-report-may-2026); [MER Feb 2021](https://forums.eveonline.com/t/monthly-economic-report-february-2021/302188)
- **WoW gold-sink history.** Sinks began with repairs and riding training (flying originally cost 5,000g). Cataclysm added service sinks (reforging, transmogrification, void storage). Later came luxury sinks such as the 5-million-gold Brutosaur mount. Forum commenters say the Brutosaur only worked because of its large practical advantage to the already-rich. Token price is said to have risen from about 30k gold at its April 2015 launch to the low-to-mid 200k range by mid-2026; that comes from one secondary blog and is unverified. [snippet] — [Engadget, WoW Archivist: history of gold sinks](https://www.engadget.com/2012-07-06-wow-archivist-an-expensive-history-of-gold-sinks.html); [growthscribe inflation history](https://growthscribe.com/from-214748-gold-to-nearly-10-million-a-short-history-of-wows-inflation/)
- **Fixed vs. percentage sinks.** A Medium essay citing Albion: "Fixed sinks (a 100-gold potion) become meaningless once players hold millions. Percentage-based sinks (transaction taxes, value-indexed repair costs) remain effective across the entire lifecycle." [snippet; secondary, blog-level] — [Medium, Designing Game Economies](https://medium.com/@msahinn21/designing-game-economies-inflation-resource-management-and-balance-fa1e6c894670)
- **OSRS Grand Exchange tax.** It started at 1% on 9 Dec 2021 and rose to 2% on 29 May 2025, capped at 5M coins per item. Sales under 50gp pay no tax because of rounding, and Bonds are exempt. Part of the tax revenue buys regulated items off the GE and deletes them (the "item sink"). The rest of the taxed gold simply disappears. [snippet] — [OSRS Wiki, GE](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Tax); [OSRS Wiki, Update: GE Tax & Item Sink](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Update:Grand_Exchange_Tax_&_Item_Sink); [Yahoo/GamesRadar coverage](https://tech.yahoo.com/gaming/articles/defying-british-dev-behind-mmos-210643395.html). An arXiv paper (2210.07970) treats the tax as a natural experiment, but its findings were not retrievable — [arXiv 2210.07970](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.07970.pdf)
- **Albion.** Sinks include listing setup fees, sales tax and buy-order fees. Repair cost is indexed to a hidden "Item Value", not market price, and quality does not change it. Full-loot PvP destroys items. Black Market NPC buy orders take a cut (snippet example: about 20%). Sources are fan or seller sites; no official Sandbox docs were found. [snippet] — [Albion Free Market, repair costs](https://albionfreemarket.com/articles/view/how-albion-online-repair-costs-are-calculated); [itemku silver guide](https://www.itemku.com/en/blog/albion-online/albion-silver-for-beginners-what-is-silver-used-for/3946/); [Nerd Lodge Black Market](https://nerdlodge.com/albion-online-black-market); [Sportskeeda gold-market fees](https://www.sportskeeda.com/mmo/gold-markets-albion-online-purchase-fees-trading)
- **Machinations / Dormans patterns.** Appendix B of Adams & Dormans, *Game Mechanics: Advanced Game Design*, lists Static Engine, Dynamic Engine, Converter Engine, Engine Building, Static Friction, Dynamic Friction and Stopping Mechanism, among others. [snippet] — [Peachpit store page](https://www.peachpit.com/store/game-mechanics-advanced-game-design-9780321820273); [O'Reilly App. B](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/game-mechanics-advanced/9780132946728/app02.html)
  - **Static Engine:** "Produces a steady flow of resources over time for players to consume or to collect." [snippet] — [O'Reilly ch.4/App B](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/game-mechanics-advanced/9780132946728/ch04.html)
  - **Converter Engine:** "a more complicated mechanism than most other engines but also offers more opportunities to improve the engine. As a result, a converter engine is nearly always dynamic." [snippet, secondary summary] — [Internal Economy Design Patterns summary](https://library-sigma-mauve.vercel.app/details/game-design/mechanics/Internal-Economy-Design-Patterns)
  - **Friction (general):** "Drains resources from the economy, reduces its productivity or both. It represents loss." **Static Friction:** a drain automatically consumes resources the player produces. [snippet, secondary] — same source
  - **Dynamic Friction (thesis):** a drain that automatically consumes player-produced resources, "the consumption rate is affected by the state of other elements in the game". The thesis says to use it "to balance games where resources are produced too fast". It "is a good way to balance any pattern that causes positive feedback, and often is part of the multiple feedback pattern". In the ch.6 lock-and-key example it complements a dynamic engine. [snippet] — [Dormans, Engineering Emergence (UvA PDF)](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/1167817/102082_thesis.pdf); [ILLC eprint](https://eprints.illc.uva.nl/id/eprint/2118/1/DS-2012-12.text.pdf); [vdoc mirror](https://vdoc.pub/documents/engineering-emergence-applied-theory-for-game-design-phd-thesis-5m85bblskpd0)
  - **Stopping Mechanism:** intent "Reduce the effectiveness of a mechanism every time it is activated." The motivation text was truncated in the snippet. **Dynamic Engine** note: "This can be balanced by using an appropriate discounting factor that indicates long term investment has diminishing returns." **Engine Building** "counters a production mechanism by periodically consuming resources." [snippet] — [Peachpit ch.5 sample PDF](https://ptgmedia.pearsoncmg.com/imprint_downloads/peachpit/peachpit/samplechapters/0321820274/0321820274_gamemechanics_ch05section.pdf); [Internal Economy Design Patterns summary](https://library-sigma-mauve.vercel.app/details/game-design/mechanics/Internal-Economy-Design-Patterns)
  - **Diminishing Returns** (a separate pattern catalog): "The returns for similar investments decrease as the player progresses in the game." [snippet] — [Eliens pattern page](https://math.vu.nl/~eliens/demo/pattern-diminishingreturns.html)

### Inferences
- In a single-player game, "inflation" does not mean prices rising. It means **the marginal star stops buying anything meaningful**: the stockpile piles up and sits unused, which is the UO "hoarding" drain. So the design goal is *a demand curve that never saturates*, not a price level.
- Endless demand without new content can only come from three sources:
  1. **Intrinsic consumption**: the currency is the tool (PoE). See §4.
  2. **State-dependent cost (dynamic friction)**: price scales with how much the player has built or tuned. This is non-penal when framed as a *price*, not as upkeep. Upkeep or decay is static/dynamic friction framed as loss, which the user forbids.
  3. **Per-use escalation (stopping mechanism / diminishing returns)**: each repeat costs more (D3 enchant, Rogue Legacy, the Satisfactory coupon curve). This keeps the marginal unit valuable forever, but if it is unbounded it eventually feels punitive (see §2).
- The user's no-penalty rule removes the whole "friction = loss" family: upkeep, decay, durability, repair, death loss and full loot. What remains is **voluntary converters**. In Machinations terms, 별의 조각 should feed a *converter* into a non-throughput resource, with dynamic pricing tied to factory state, so demand grows as the factory grows. The price must not *gate* growth, or factory size becomes tied to anime time.
- The OSRS GE item sink, where tax revenue buys and deletes items, is a two-currency coupling: one currency's sink removes surplus from another pool. That is analogous to stars being the fee that "settles" or converts something.

## 2. Systemic sinks that scale automatically, and which avoid bad feelings

### Takeaway
Auto-scaling sinks come in several shapes: proportional upkeep, value-indexed fees, escalating per-roll costs, global escalating unlock costs, and point-to-coupon converters with rising thresholds. The recurring player complaints are about **(a) upkeep and decay as loss** and **(b) cross-coupled or unbounded escalation that punishes choice**. The best-tolerated version is a *voluntary, per-target escalation with a visible curve and no loss of what is already owned*. Satisfactory's AWESOME Sink is the closest factory-game example of an endless converter.

### Cited Findings
- **Satisfactory AWESOME Sink.** It destroys inserted items and converts them into points "based on their value or complexity". Points are global across all sinks and persist even if sinks are removed. Coupons can be printed from any sink and printing needs no power. "with every coupon unlocked the next coupon requires more points", while per-item point values never decrease. The wiki estimates 1,895 coupons to buy every non-producible shop item, which would require 125,721,388,500 points. The early-coupon cost curve has been revised over time. [snippet] — [Satisfactory Wiki, AWESOME Sink](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/AWESOME_Sink); [Talk page](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Talk:AWESOME_Sink); [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/526870/discussions/0/3801649759599050781); [FICSIT Coupon](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/FICSIT_Coupon)
- **Diablo 3 Mystic enchanting.** It rerolls one stat for salvage materials plus gold, and the gold price rises with each re-enchant on the same item, with "no discernible cap" except on rings and amulets. A pre-launch preview gave increments of 15k–30k per roll and expected "a great gold sink… numbers still need a bit of tuning". Player reports: over 2M gold after about 10 rolls; "I blew 30 mil before I knew it". [snippet; fan blog/forums] — [postmasterinterest blog](https://postmasterinterest.blogspot.com/2014/05/diablo-3-lets-talk-about-mystic.html); [PC Gamer, Mystic as AH replacement](https://pcgamer.com/diablo-3-reaper-of-souls-your-auction-house-replacement-is-the-mystic-2)
- **Diablo 4 occult affix reroll** uses the same escalation: "first reroll is 30k, soon it's 300k, then 1m then 3m, then 10m+". Guides advise a per-item budget cap. D4 forums also complain about an 11M+ gold Paragon respec. [snippet] — [Mobalytics occult reroll guide](https://mobalytics.gg/blog/diablo-4/occult-affix-rerolling-guide/); [D4 forum](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/d4/t/11-million-gold-to-respec-paragon-are-you-kidding/14928?page=49)
- **Diablo 4 gold glut.** Reports of players "swimming in gold" and begging for a bailout, and of a Season Blessing that raised income but also costs. This shows that scaling income with scaling costs can feel like a treadmill. [snippet] — [Yahoo/Kotaku](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/swimming-gold-months-diablo-iv-162900621.html); [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/diablo-4-season-blessing-intended-to-earn-you-more-gold-also-makes-things-cost-more/1100-6516322/)
- **Rogue Legacy (1 and 2).** "Buying a castle upgrade or rune causes the cost of every other one to increase." This is cross-coupled global escalation. A Rogue Legacy 2 forum post calls it the game's biggest grievance. It suggests per-upgrade exponential costs instead, or grouping upgrades by theme so a purchase raises costs only within its group. In RL2, Charon takes all leftover gold before the next run, which forces a spend-everything cadence. [snippet] — [TV Tropes RL](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/RogueLegacy); [Steam RL2 thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1253920/discussions/0/2987537248800464833); [Digital Trends RL2 guide](https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/rogue-legacy-2-beginners-guide); [Magnetic Mag](https://magneticmag.com/2022/06/five-tips-for-rogue-legacy-2/)
- **Value-indexed fees** (Albion repair by hidden Item Value; percentage-based transaction taxes) scale with player wealth automatically. See §1 citations. [snippet]
- **Upkeep and decay** (UO house decay, MMO housing taxes) work as sinks but are explicitly loss-framed. See the Engadget citation in §1. [snippet]
- **Crafting with variance** (PoE chaos spam): "most crafting techniques are costly and unpredictable, so only the most successful crafting attempts are cost-efficient". "Bricking" is when an orb adds only the corrupted status or fully rerolls a good item. Bricking is a failure state. [snippet] — [PoEWiki Craft](https://www.poewiki.net/wiki/Craft); [Craft of Exile basics](https://craftofexile.com/basics)
- **PoE2 removed Orbs of Scouring** ("you cannot just erase unwanted modifiers and try again") and has no duplicate-affix protection. A Steam post says crafting "in its current state is discouraging play". This is anecdotal evidence that harsh variance sinks drive negative feeling. [snippet] — [PoE Vault PoE2 mistake](https://www.poe-vault.com/poe2/news/this-poe2-crafting-mistake-could-ruin-your-best-items); [Steam PoE2](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2694490/discussions/0/592897598382226123)

### Inferences (feeling analysis for the no-penalty constraint)
| Sink shape | Endless? | Scales with build? | Negative-feeling risk | Fit for 별의 조각 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upkeep proportional to holdings | yes | yes | high (loss, "rent") | **forbidden** |
| Decay/durability/repair | yes | yes | high | **forbidden** |
| Transaction/market fee | yes (needs a market) | with wealth | low–medium | N/A in single-player |
| Consumable per repeatable activity | yes | with activity rate | low if optional; high if it gates the main loop | risky: gating = binds factory to anime time |
| Escalating per-target reroll (D3/D4) | yes | with number of targets | medium (unbounded escalation feels like a trap) | good **if** non-destructive and the curve is capped or soft |
| Global cross-coupled escalation (Rogue Legacy) | yes | with total purchases | high (punishes choice; top complaint) | avoid; use per-group escalation |
| Converter with rising thresholds (AWESOME Sink) | yes | with total input | low (points never lost, value never decreases) | strong template |
| Variance crafting with bricking (PoE) | yes | with targets | high (failure) | only the variance-without-loss subset |

- A **non-destructive reroll** (preview the new result and keep or revert, or choose 1 of N) keeps PoE's endless-optimization appeal while removing bricking. Each roll is still consumed, so the sink stays endless; nothing the player owns gets worse, so there is no penalty. [inference; D3 enchant also presents a choice that includes the original stat — **training knowledge, unverified**]
- **Per-target soft escalation**: cost rises the more you tune *one* machine and resets per machine. Demand then scales with factory size (more machines means more cheap first rolls) without gating size. Stars are never required to *place* a machine, only to *refine* one.
- AWESOME-Sink logic, inverted: there, factory output becomes points that buy shop items. For 별의 조각, the external currency (stars) is spent as the "complexity" input to a converter whose rising threshold is never-ending, so the marginal star always moves a visible bar.

### Gaps
- Exact AWESOME Sink coupon cost formula: the wiki "Coupon cost" section was not retrievable.
- No developer statement found on why Rogue Legacy couples all upgrade prices.
- No academic data on player affect toward escalating reroll costs; evidence is forum anecdotes only.

## 3. Dual-currency designs where the second currency comes from a different activity

### Takeaway
Successful secondary currencies (Nook Miles, Qi Gems, Hades' Darkness/Gems/Keys, Dead Cells' cells) share three traits. They are earned by a *different verb* than the main currency. They are **not purchasable with the main currency**, a one-way firewall. And each feeds **its own dedicated system**. Their weakness for this project is that most of them spend into a *finite* catalogue (shop items, blueprints), so they go dead once the catalogue is exhausted. Hades and Animal Crossing paper over this with exchanges or vouchers back into other currencies.

### Cited Findings
- **Animal Crossing: New Horizons, Nook Miles.** Earned through on-island activities and milestones (selling, collecting, fishing, bug catching, even opening the NookPhone). Spent at Resident Services and Nook Stop. Bells cover "essentially everything else – furniture, clothing, renovations". **No Bells→Miles trade**, though buying with Bells indirectly advances milestones. Miles→Bells exists only via the Bell Voucher (500 Miles → sells for 3,000 Bells). Guides disagree on which currency matters more late-game ("Bells eventually become more necessary"). [snippet] — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/animal-crossing-bells-nook-miles-which-is-better/); [Gamepressure ACNH](https://www.gamepressure.com/animal-crossing-new-horizons/bells-nook-miles/z1d1f6); [GameRant Bell Voucher](https://gamerant.com/animal-crossing-new-horizons-bell-voucher-what-is-convert-miles/)
- **Stardew Valley, Qi Gems.** Only obtainable after unlocking Qi's Walnut Room. Sources: Qi Special Orders, the Shrine of Challenge, and exchanging leftover Golden Walnuts (up to 28 gems). **Cannot be bought with gold** (unlike casino Qi Coins). Spent in the Walnut Room shop and vending machine. [snippet] — [Stardew Wiki Qi Gem](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Qi_Gem); [Stardew Wiki Walnut Room](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Qi%27s_Walnut_Room); [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/how-to-unlock-mr-qis-walnut-room-vending-machine-perfection-and-special-orders-guide/)
- **Hades meta-currencies.** Each currency feeds one NPC or system:
  - Darkness → Mirror of Night talents, kept after death. **Respec costs one Chthonic Key and refunds all Darkness**, so spending is reversible.
  - Chthonic Keys → unlock weapons and Mirror slots. Once everything is unlocked they can be traded at the Wretched Broker.
  - Gemstones → House Contractor renovations (snippet sources disagree on whether these are mostly cosmetic house items or functional map upgrades such as healing rooms or challenge chests).
  - Nectar → gifts for affinity and Keepsakes.
  - Diamonds → rarer, from bosses or the Broker.
  - The Wretched Broker converts rare currencies into each other (e.g., Ambrosia ↔ Titan Blood).
  [snippet] — [Gamepressure Hades](https://www.gamepressure.com/hades/charons-obol-nectar-and-darkness/z6d9c6); [Prima Games items guide](https://primagames.com/gaming/hades-items-guide-nectars-keys-ambrosia-titan-blood-and-more/2)
- **Dead Cells, cells.** A special currency "mainly for unlocks", **lost on death** (a penalty). Spent at the Collector to unlock blueprinted items (weapons, skills, mutations, upgrades, outfits), or at the Blacksmith to raise the odds of higher-quality gear. Blueprints themselves must be carried to the Collector, then unlocked with cells. Example costs: 40 cells (Tesla Coil), and 80, 100 or 200 for others. One wiki count: 47 Always, 20 Normal, 40 Rare and 3 Legendary drop blueprints. Unlocked items **join the random pool**, and players worry that unlocking everything "dilutes your weapons pool with worse weapons". [snippet] — [Dead Cells Wiki, Cells](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Cells); [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/588650/discussions/0/1750106661716473499); [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/dead-cells/hero-development-and-cells/z49ebd)
- **Rogue Legacy gold.** Spent on manor upgrades with rising costs, and in RL2 any unspent gold is taken by Charon. See §2. [snippet]
- **Diablo 3 crafting materials.** Salvage mats plus gold for enchanting. See §2. [snippet]
- **Monster Hunter.** I found no sourced material in this session. **[training knowledge, unverified]**: MH spends part-specific monster materials on gear crafting and upgrading, and uses separate point currencies (e.g., Research/Kamura points) for consumables and services. Its gear catalogue is finite per title.

### Inferences
- The **one-way firewall** (main currency cannot buy the secondary) is what makes the secondary currency *meaningful*. Qi Gems and Nook Miles both do this, and 별의 조각 already does by construction (anime-only faucet).
- The **failure mode** of every example is a *finite sink catalogue*. Qi shop items, Dead Cells blueprints and Hades Keys all saturate, after which the currency is converted (Broker, Bell Voucher, walnuts→gems) or dies. That conversion is a patch, not endless demand. For "endless without content", 별의 조각 must spend into a **parametric** system (rerolls, tuning, rising thresholds), not a **catalogue**.
- Hades' **refundable Darkness respec** is a strong anti-regret pattern. Spending never feels like loss, yet the currency is still needed to *add* capacity. That fits "든든".
- Dead Cells shows that **breadth unlocks can be net-negative** (pool dilution) unless the player can toggle them. Any breadth system fed by stars should let players enable or disable unlocked options.
- Nook Miles shows a secondary currency can be "needed" early and fade late. To avoid that, the star system should scale with the factory (dynamic), not with early-game milestones.

### Gaps
- No primary-source designer commentary (Supergiant, ConcernedApe, Nintendo, Motion Twin) on why these currencies were separated.
- Monster Hunter unsourced this session.

## 4. The Path of Exile model, and whether a "modify/tune" sink can work for factory machines without changing throughput math

### Takeaway
PoE's own developer diary states the core principle. Gold needs "many artificial sinks that do not have any real gameplay benefit"; making every currency item useful for improving a character means "each currency item is its own sink". Value lasts because the target space (item modifiers) is combinatorial and the optimization never ends. A factory analog is plausible if the reroll or tune space is **topological or behavioural, not quantitative**: footprint, port faces, rotation and chaining. Those change *how easily a design fits*, not *how much it produces*.

### Cited Findings
- **GGG Dev Diary "Rethinking Gold as a Currency" (Feb 2011):** "In order to make gold a viable currency, the developers created many artificial sinks that do not have any real gameplay benefit… By having every currency item inherently useful to improve a character, each currency item is its own sink. In our experience with Path of Exile, a majority of currency item trades end with the item being immediately consumed by the recipient." The diary also says repair costs could be dropped entirely. [snippet] — [PoE forum dev diary](https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/55102)
- **Brian Weissman (GGG co-founder):** "You can introduce various gold sinks like durability or whatever, but generally you're just creating solutions to a problem that doesn't need to exist." [snippet] — [TopTierTactics interview](https://www.toptiertactics.com/13179/path-of-exile-interview-with-grinding-gear-games-brian-weissman/)
- **Official game page:** "removing gold as a currency and basing our trade economy around orbs that can randomly reroll the properties of other items." [snippet] — [pathofexile.com/game](https://www.pathofexile.com/game)
- The Chaos Orb is the reference unit of value. [snippet] — [PoE forum currency guide](https://pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/2580682); [PoEWiki Currency](https://www.poewiki.net/wiki/Currency)
- **Drawback:** the most-traded currency tends to lose its crafting role over time (Chaos Orbs in PoE1), so periodic rebalancing or a sequel reset is needed. In PoE2, Gold was introduced as the NPC-trader currency and orbs act more like crafting materials. [snippet; personal analysis site plus guides] — [currency-as-crafting analysis](https://saschb2b.github.io/game-mechanics/concepts/currency-as-crafting); [Mobalytics PoE2 currency](https://mobalytics.gg/poe-2/guides/currency-guide)
- **Variance and optimization:** Craft of Exile exists to simulate crafting distributions and total cost in chaos, which shows players treat it as an endless optimization problem. [snippet] — [Craft of Exile](https://craftofexile.com/basics)
- **Bricking and PoE2 harshness** (failure-state risk): see §2. [snippet]

### Inferences: a factory "tuning orb" sink without throughput change (hypotheses for the report writer, not sourced facts)
- **Why PoE works, in one sentence:** an endless, combinatorial, player-chosen target space, plus a consumable that moves you through it, plus diminishing chance of improvement as you near your ideal. The last part is a natural stopping mechanism, so demand never saturates and stockpiles stay "든든" because one more roll might be the one.
- **Throughput-neutral trait axes for a Create-style machine.** Ratios and items per minute stay untouched on all of these:
  - **Footprint shape** (e.g., 2×1 ↔ L-shape ↔ 1×2 vertical) with the same volume and recipe speed.
  - **Port faces**: which side is input or output, top vs. side, or swapping the shaft-input side.
  - **Rotation direction** (CW/CCW) requirement, and whether a machine passes rotation through (shaft pass-through), so gearboxes are needed or not.
  - **Chaining / adjacency trait**: e.g., "can share a belt face with a neighbour" or "can be stacked", a pure layout affordance.
  - **Priority or filter logic slot**: which input it pulls first, or a round-robin vs. fill-first output. This changes routing behaviour, not totals.
  - **Noise, particles, colour-coding**: pure cosmetic, already rejected as "cosmetics", so these should be excluded or folded in as free side-effects only.
- **Why this is "genuinely needed" without binding factory size:** every machine is placeable and fully productive with default traits. Traits make *specific layouts* possible or compact: a tight monster-farm corridor, a vertical tower, removing a gearbox. Demand scales with factory ambition (more machines, more compact designs). Anime time never caps *quantity*, only how elegantly the build can be packed. The risk to flag: if compactness effectively equals more machines per plot, footprint traits indirectly raise output per land. Mitigation: keep the volume and tile count constant across shapes, or make traits orientation-only.
- **Remove bricking (the no-penalty rule):**
  - Show the reroll result as a *candidate*; the player keeps the old one or takes the new, and the star is spent either way.
  - Or "pick 1 of 3".
  - Or a deterministic "lock one trait, reroll the rest" at a higher cost. PoE's crafting bench metamods are the inspiration, but PoE bench mechanics were not sourced this session.
- **Self-balancing:** cost per roll can escalate per machine instance (D3 style) but softly. Better is a *pity* curve that guarantees a target trait after N rolls, which avoids the D3/D4 "10M+" trap and keeps the experience non-punitive.
- **Template/blueprint reuse:** if Create-style schematics let players copy a tuned machine, tuning becomes a one-time cost per design, which threatens endlessness. Option: a tuned trait is per placed instance, and copying a schematic copies the *request*, which still consumes stars per instance. This is a design decision to flag, since players may see it as a tax.

### Gaps
- No sourced example of a factory or automation game that monetizes layout traits such as footprint or port rerolls. This appears to be a novel combination. Satisfactory's AWESOME Sink is the closest endless converter, but it buys catalogue items. The one adjacent mechanic is Factorio Space Age "quality", which **does** affect stats, and it was not sourced this session.
- GGG's own long-term data on currency value retention (beyond the dev-diary claim) was not found.

## 5. Hades-style multiple meta-currencies, with stars feeding one permanent "breadth" system

### Takeaway
Hades shows that splitting meta-progression into several currencies, each owned by one system and one NPC, lets each currency stay legible and "needed". It also shows the breadth-unlock systems (Keys for weapons and aspects) saturate. Hades adds two soft valves: a free refund of Darkness on respec, and a Broker that converts surplus. For an endless breadth system, the stars should buy **options that combine**, such as trait slots, loadouts or toggles, rather than a fixed list of unlocks.

### Cited Findings
- Hades Darkness is spent on Mirror of Night talents, persists through death, and is fully refunded on respec for one Key. Keys unlock weapons and Mirror slots and become tradeable once everything is unlocked. Gems pay for House Contractor renovations. Nectar is used for gifts. The Broker converts rare currencies. [snippet] — [Gamepressure Hades](https://www.gamepressure.com/hades/charons-obol-nectar-and-darkness/z6d9c6); [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/gaming/hades-items-guide-nectars-keys-ambrosia-titan-blood-and-more/2)
- Dead Cells: breadth unlocks enter the random pool, and players worry about dilution. [snippet] — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/588650/discussions/0/1750106661716473499)
- Rogue Legacy: globally coupled price escalation across breadth and power unlocks is the most-cited grievance. Grouped escalation is the player-proposed fix. [snippet] — [Steam RL2 thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1253920/discussions/0/2987537248800464833)
- Satisfactory: the AWESOME Shop's catalogue is large (1,895 coupons to clear) but finite. After that, points have no use, though coupons can also buy producible items repeatedly (wiki phrasing "non-producible items" implies producible items remain buyable). [snippet] — [Satisfactory Wiki](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/AWESOME_Sink)

### Inferences
- **"Breadth" that never saturates:**
  - (a) **Slots, not items.** Stars buy additional *concurrent* trait or behaviour slots on machines, or additional saved layout "presets". Each new slot multiplies combinations, so content stays fixed while choice grows.
  - (b) **Per-instance tuning** (§4). Breadth comes from how many different configurations exist across the factory.
  - (c) **A refundable respec** (Hades Darkness) so spending is never regretted.
- **One currency, one system, one NPC.** Following Hades, 별의 조각 should own exactly one permanent system, e.g., a "machine tuning workshop" or a "star forge" NPC. That keeps the currency's identity clear and its need legible. Mixing it into land, monsters or gifts (already rejected) blurs it.
- **No Broker escape valve back into the main currency.** The Bell Voucher and Hades Broker exist because their catalogues end. If the star sink is parametric and endless, no conversion valve is needed. Adding one (stars → factory resources) would create a back door that ties factory size to anime time, so it should be avoided.
- **Stockpile psychology (든든).** Stockpiles feel reassuring when (1) there is always a next use worth making, (2) spending is never forced on a timer (unlike RL2's Charon) and (3) spent value is never lost (Hades refund, non-destructive reroll). Each of these maps to a sourced counter-example above: RL2 Charon forces spending, PoE bricking loses value, Dead Cells loses currency on death.

- No sourced data on player sentiment toward refundable respecs vs. permanent spends.
