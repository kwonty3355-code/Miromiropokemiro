# Hoarding / Stockpile Psychology and "Holding Is Valuable" Mechanics (든든함 research)

Research method note: WebFetch to gamedeveloper.com and wiki.leagueoflegends.com was blocked (host not resolvable in this session). Most findings below come from search-result snippets and are marked [snippet]. Facts from community wikis/guides are marked as such. Today: 2026-11-26.

Context reminder for the report writer: currency "별의 조각" is earned only in eyes-free anime mode. Target feeling on seeing a large pile: 든든하다 (secure/reassured), NOT "I should have collected more" (regret/FOMO) and NOT "too much, when will I spend it" (useless surplus). Must be genuinely needed, not touch factory math, endlessly useful, non-inflationary, low content cost. Already rejected: convenience-only, cosmetics, gacha, monster lures/gifts/storage, land expansion merge, time-skips, wish events, mega projects, retry/second-chance insurance.

## 1. Psychology: why stockpiles feel good, and when they feel bad

### Takeaway
Players hoard because spending feels like loss (loss aversion, option-value, endowment effect) and because a reserve converts uncertainty into a felt sense of safety; the feeling turns sour when the stockpile has no outlet (TV Tropes "Money for Nothing") or when it is never used and the player loses because of it ("Too Awesome to Use"). The 든든함 the user wants is the *option value* side of hoarding, without the *never-spent* or *useless surplus* side.

### Cited Findings
- **Too Awesome to Use / Elixir syndrome.** TV Tropes defines it as items useful but rarely worth spending, saved "for the final boss"; players often hold consumables so long they lose survivable fights. The RPG subpage gives the example of players entering the final boss with ~102 stones of hoarded healing items, and lists Elfin elixirs (full MP restore) as a classic example. Epigraph: "By that argument, your Majesty will never use it until it is too late." — [TV Tropes: Too Awesome to Use](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/TooAwesometoUse); [TV Tropes: Too Awesome to Use / RPG](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/TooAwesomeToUse/RolePlayingGames) [snippet]
- Related tropes: "Emergency Energy Tank" is a sub-trope of Too Awesome to Use (a reserve whose whole value is being there when needed); "Anti-Hoarding" covers design limits on stockpiling (e.g., a party can only carry two Health Potions per recruited member). — [TV Tropes: Emergency Energy Tank](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/EmergencyEnergyTank); [TV Tropes: Anti-Hoarding (Playing With)](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/PlayingWith/AntiHoarding) [snippet]
- **Hoarding is economic, per a Game Developer article ("Avoiding the hoarder trap").** Defines hoarding as withholding items that could help now in favor of a long-term goal; argues the cause is usually economic: scarce/expensive items get saved, cheap/plentiful ones get used. Proposed mitigation: make consumables plentiful enough to use in any battle. — [Game Developer: Avoiding the hoarder trap](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/avoiding-the-hoarder-trap-in-game-design) [snippet; full fetch blocked]
- **Loss aversion and option value.** A Game Developer analysis argues loss aversion produces a strong pull against giving up options, even ones a player never expects to use; example: Fallout 3 / New Vegas unlimited container storage leads players to pile gear they'll never need. Cites Shin & Ariely's Duke "disappearing doors" experiment, where participants kept paying to keep doors open they did not plan to use. — [Game Developer: Option availability and loss aversion](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/analysis-option-availability-and-loss-aversion-in-game-design) [snippet]
- A designer newsletter frames consumable use as cost weighed against risk of losing a fight: players stockpile when they fear a fight might be unwinnable without the item; recharging resources (e.g., regenerating shields) are cited as a less hoard-prone alternative; features requiring permanently giving something up are often avoided entirely, and reframing the same mechanic changes reception. — [Bottomfeeder: Understanding the Player Brain Pt.1 Loss Avoidance](https://bottomfeeder.substack.com/p/understanding-the-player-brain-pt) [snippet]
- **Research caveat:** an HCI (Univ. of Saskatchewan) paper notes there are reasons to expect loss aversion to be weaker in games and reasons to expect it to persist; they built a game to test it — i.e., it is not settled. — [HCI Usask PDF](https://hci.usask.ca/wp-content/cache/mendeley-file-cache/6eb59aeb-38be-37ad-950c-7c625cb4cce2.pdf) [snippet]
- **Endowment effect.** Munro & Ferreira De Sousa (J. Economic Psychology, 2012) used RuneScape as a testbed; with purely virtual goods they replicated that more trade-experienced individuals show less status-quo behavior (could not exclude selection). Drouvelis & Sonnemans (European Economic Review, 2017) found robust endowment effects in strategic games, larger in games than in lotteries. — [RePEc: Munro & Ferreira De Sousa](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:joepsy:v:33:y:2012:i:3:p:482-493); [IDEAS: The endowment effect in games](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/eecrev/v94y2017icp240-262.html) [snippet]
- **Player motivations.** Quantic Foundry's model has 12 motivations (Destruction, Excitement, Competition, Community, Challenge, Strategy, Completion, Power, Fantasy, Story, Design, Discovery). "Completion" = finish everything, every collectible; "Power" = start weak, level/upgrade path; the two often co-occur. Completion is reported as the most age-stable motivation and "always in the top 3" across gender/age segments. There is no separate "accumulation" motivation in the model. — [Quantic Foundry Gamer Motivation Model Reference PDF](https://quanticfoundry.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Gamer-Motivation-Model-Reference.pdf); [Quantic Foundry home](https://quanticfoundry.com/); [Game Developer survey piece](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/survey-men-play-games-to-compete-women-play-to-complete) [snippet]
- **"Number go up".** Idle/incremental games' design philosophy "can be summarized as numbers go up"; Tuts+ ties it to Skinner-box periodic reward and argues weak human numeracy lets people enjoy numbers without external meaning; an academic paper notes idle games are "primarily played by not playing"; a CHI PLAY 2019 paper interviewed six idle-game designers. — [Goodreads blog: Numbers go up](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/25058315-numbers-go-up?tab=book); [Tuts+: Numbers getting bigger](https://code.tutsplus.com/numbers-getting-bigger-what-are-incremental-games-and-why-are-they-fun--cms-23925a); [NSF PAR paper](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10174274) [snippet]
- **Preparedness / survival.** Evidence is thin. A psychologist quoted by Big Think says prepping is "not just about stockpiling supplies, it's about the emotional charge" (adrenaline, sense of purpose, distraction from discomfort), with a crash that restarts the cycle; stockpiling was mediated by social learning (seeing others panic-buy). A DayZ player survey (n>7,000) found 68% described the game as mildly/very cathartic (mastering threats). In a 7DRL dragon-lair roguelike ("Greedy Rogue"), a reviewer observed that "having more money results in less anxiety" — gold acting as a survival/comfort stat. — [Big Think: Doomsday preppers philosophy](https://bigthink.com/thinking/doomsday-preppers-philosophy/); [JSTOR Daily: therapeutic value of horror games](https://daily.jstor.org/the-therapeutic-value-of-horror-video-games); [itch.io 7DRL 2022 entry](https://itch.io/jam/7drl-challenge-2022/rate/1436113) [snippet]
- **When stockpiles feel bad: Money for Nothing.** TV Tropes: money easy to earn with nothing worthwhile to buy; typical when money only buys one-time purchases and becomes useless once all are bought. Developers respond with a sudden late expensive obstacle or with Money Sinks, which exist mostly to prevent the trope. — [TV Tropes: Money for Nothing](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/MoneyForNothing); [TV Tropes: Money Sink](https://TVTropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/MoneySink) [snippet]

### Inferences
- Two distinct "bad" feelings map to the user's two failure modes: (a) "I should have collected more" = regret/loss framing; arises when the pile's value is only realized by spending and the spend target is bigger than the pile (shortfall salience). (b) "Too much, when will I spend it" = Money for Nothing; arises when the pile exceeds every available sink. 든든함 sits between them: the pile is *already doing work* (no shortfall) and *more pile still does a little more work* (no dead surplus).
- Hoarding research suggests the comfort comes from *option value under uncertainty*. If the game has no uncertainty for the reserve to buffer, the reserve cannot feel reassuring. So 별의 조각 needs something it quietly buffers or guards against — without becoming the rejected "retry/second-chance insurance" (i.e., the value should be continuous/ambient, not a consumed revive).
- The cleanest way to dissolve Too-Awesome-to-Use is to make holding *itself* the use (no consumption decision at all). That avoids elixir syndrome entirely because the player never has to decide "is this the moment to spend?".
- Bottomfeeder's "permanent give-up is avoided" point suggests that if any spending of 별의 조각 exists, it should feel like converting/placing rather than losing (e.g., the pile remains visible/counted).

### Gaps
- Bartle's taxonomy (Achievers/Explorers/Socializers/Killers) was not searched/verified here; from background knowledge, Bartle has no "hoarder" type — collectors usually fall under Achievers. Treat as unverified.
- No peer-reviewed study found that directly measures "sense of security from in-game stockpiles". The Game Developer full articles (hoarder trap; collecting psychology "Analysis: The Psychology Behind Item Collecting And Achievement Hoarding") could not be fetched; only snippets used.
- No source located on Minecraft storage rooms / Factorio buffer chests as a pride/security phenomenon; omitted rather than invented.

## 2. Mechanics where simply HOLDING a resource is valuable (and how they cap it)

### Takeaway
The proven pattern is "passive yield or passive power proportional to holdings, with a hard cap or step-thresholds" (TFT, Balatro, Isaac Money=Power, Animal Crossing bank, Cookie Clicker Lucky). Caps keep it balanced and create a clear "full/safe" line players aim to sit at — which is exactly a 든든함 marker. The inverse pattern (holding is risk: Dark Souls souls, RimWorld wealth -> bigger raids, Kingdom pouch overflow) creates tension instead of comfort.

### Cited Findings
- **Teamfight Tactics interest.** +1 gold per 10 gold held at round end, capped at +5 (reached at 50 gold); 50 vs 70 gold gives the same interest. Guides advise spending down to 50 each turn (e.g., buy XP); sitting far above 50 is "hoarding past the point where gold could have improved your board". Riot's rationale for the specific 50 cap was not found; a Dot Esports piece reports Riot judged there was "too much gold flowing through the game" (loss-streak income, unit sales), not directly tied to the cap. — [Mobalytics TFT econ guide](https://mobalytics.gg/blog/how-to-manage-your-economy-in-teamfight-tactics-three-strategies/); [Metabot TFT economy explained](https://metabot.gg/en/TFT/guides/tft-economy-gold-interest-streaks-explained); [Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/?p=228565) [snippet; LoL wiki fetch blocked]
- **Balatro interest.** $1 per $5 held at round end, capped at $5 (reached at $25). Seed Money voucher raises cap to $10 ($50 held); Money Tree raises to $20 ($100 held) and unlocks after maxing interest 10 consecutive rounds, appearing only after Seed Money is bought. Joker "To the Moon" adds another $1 per $5; with both vouchers the cap can reach $40/round. One source claims $6/$7 caps — conflicts with all others, likely error. — [games.gg Balatro economy](https://games.gg/balatro/guides/balatro-economy-guide/); [Switchblade Gaming](https://www.switchbladegaming.com/strategy-games/balatro-economy-strategy/); [TrueAchievements Nest Egg](https://www.trueachievements.com/a424926/nest-egg-achievement); [Balatro wiki: To the Moon](https://balatrogame.fandom.com/wiki/To_the_Moon); conflicting: [saschb2b shop economy](https://saschb2b.github.io/game-mechanics/games/balatro/shop-economy) [snippet]
- **Binding of Isaac "Money = Power".** +0.04 damage per coin held, capped at +3.96 at 99 coins; Deep Pockets raises cap to 999 coins (+39.96). Damage formula is itself sublinear (example: 99 pennies -> ~8.4 damage on base 3.5). A talk-page user reported shortfall at high counts (unconfirmed). — [Isaac wiki: Money = Power](https://bindingofisaacrebirth.wiki.gg/wiki/Money_=_Power); [Isaac Fandom: Damage](https://bindingofisaac.fandom.com/wiki/Stats/Damage) [snippet]
- TV Tropes has a "Cast From Money" trope (money spent/used as a combat resource; "higher valued coins do more damage") and "Wallet of Holding" (carry caps). — [TV Tropes: Cast From Money](https://tvtropes.org:443/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/CastFromMoney); [TV Tropes: Wallet of Holding](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WalletOFHolding) [snippet]
- **Cookie Clicker bank-scaled reward.** Golden cookie "Lucky!" gives the larger of 15% of bank or 15 min CpS (+13); maximized when bank holds ~100 minutes (6,000 s) of CpS — community rule "ideal bank = CpS × 6000". Community guides also size the bank to absorb a "Ruin" (capped ~10 min CpS loss) so one Lucky recovers it. Wrinklers drain production (5% each, 50% at 10), and return 1.1× when popped (a held, growing reservoir). — [Cookie Clicker Fandom: Frenzy cookie](https://cookieclicker.fandom.com/wiki/File:Frenzy_cookie.png); [Advanced Ascension & Combo Guide (Google Doc)](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1q1w_AdX-ZPG7_1hXTBKXveugRPeBjaa2MKVAIQkxBek/mobilebasic) [snippet; community rules of thumb]
- **Animal Crossing: New Horizons bank.** Interest cut in April 2020 (v1.2.0) from ~0.5% to ~0.05% per month, payouts capped at 9,999 Bells/month; computed on last-day balance. Business Insider headline frames the cut as Nintendo "cut interest rate... to stimulate spending". Savings-amount reward tables (gifts at deposit milestones) exist across the series per Nookipedia, but exact NH thresholds could not be extracted. — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/nintendo-slashes-interest-rates-in-animal-crossing-new-1843019628); [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/animal-crossing-update-lowers-interest-rates-but-h/1100-6476413/); [Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/nintendo-cuts-interest-rate-animal-crossing-new-horizons-stimulate-spending-2020-4/); [Nookipedia: Savings](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Savings) [snippet]
- **Inverse: holding as risk.**
  - Dark Souls: souls held are lost on death; one recovery chance at the bloodstain; a second death erases them. Critics call it an exciting tension device; others say it is weighted too much toward risk. Ring of Life Protection prevents the loss. — [Game Developer: Heaviness of Demon's Souls](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/analysis-the-game-design-heaviness-of-i-demon-s-souls-i-); [Medium: Dark Souls Engine of Punishment](https://medium.com/power-level/dark-souls-an-engine-of-punishment-13f1d4597806); [TV Tropes: Anti-Frustration Features / Dark Souls](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/AntiFrustrationFeatures/DarkSouls) [snippet]
  - RimWorld: storyteller wealth (items + creatures + 50% buildings) drives raid points; <14k wealth has practically no effect; >1M wealth caps at 4,200 raid points; "wealth management" is a player practice of keeping wealth low; a "wealth-independent" storyteller setting removes the link. — [RimWorld Wiki: Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points); [RimWorld Wiki: Wealth management](https://mail.rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Wealth_management) [snippet; community wiki]
  - Kingdom Two Crowns: coin pouch holds ~40 coins comfortably (~50 overfilled); overflow coins fall to ground or water (one guide: 50/50); Greed attacks can knock coins off the monarch. — [Kingdom Wiki: Overflowing pouch](https://kingdomthegame.fandom.com/wiki/File:Overflowing_pouch.jpg); [grindnstrat guide](https://grindnstrat.com/?p=8032); [Wikipedia: Kingdom Two Crowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Two_Crowns) [snippet]
- **Tabletop:** Heartbreaker TTRPG has a dragon-blood mechanic: PCs grow more powerful as they hoard gold, and losing gold removes those abilities. — [Heartbreaker project page](https://zechlau14.github.io/projects/heartbreaker) [snippet]

### Inferences
- Every positive "holding" mechanic found uses a **cap or step threshold**: TFT 50, Balatro 25/50/100, Isaac 99/999, ACNH 9,999/month. Caps produce a "sweet spot" that players actively aim to sit at — the "I'm at max interest" feeling is the closest documented analogue of 든든함. The cap also defines when surplus starts being "dead", so the design must either (a) make the cap rise slowly over the long game (Balatro voucher ladder, Isaac Deep Pockets), or (b) give above-cap surplus a soft, diminishing use.
- TFT/Balatro interest pay out *more of the same currency* (compounding). For 별의 조각 that would be inflationary and bind value to anime time; the safer analogue is Isaac's Money=Power or Cookie Clicker's Lucky: the held amount scales a *non-currency* effect, so nothing compounds.
- ACNH's 2020 rate cut is a documented case of a developer reducing passive yield from holdings to push spending — evidence that pure passive yield on a large stockpile can be judged too strong / anti-spending.
- The inverse cases (Dark Souls, RimWorld, Kingdom) show holding-as-risk creates anxiety and "wealth management" minimization — the opposite of 든든함. RimWorld in particular shows that tying threat to holdings makes players *avoid* big piles. Avoid any "bigger pile attracts danger" framing.
- Cookie Clicker's "bank sized to absorb a Ruin" is a nice example of the pile as a *buffer that keeps working without being spent*: the reserve's job is to make bad events harmless; the right amount is computable relative to current scale.

### Gaps
- No Riot primary source on why the TFT interest cap is 50 (dev blogs not reachable).
- RuneScape bank value as status, Pokemon Pay Day / Amulet Coin, Hearthstone "bank" mechanics, Monopoly cash, Minecraft/Factorio storage: not researched in this pass (call budget); no sourced claims available.
- Exact ACNH savings-milestone thresholds not retrieved (Nookipedia table truncated in snippet).

## 3. Stockpile as passive aura / threshold / status / visible pile; balancing hold vs spend

### Takeaway
Holdings can provide ambient value via thresholds (tiers), diminishing-returns scaling, NPC/status reaction, or physical visibility (Dungeon Keeper gold piles); the hold-vs-spend tension is balanced in practice by caps that make "spend down to the cap" the optimal line, and by sinks that prevent dead surplus. For the user's constraints, a non-consumed, threshold/diminishing aura that scales a non-production, non-currency effect is the best-supported pattern.

### Cited Findings
- **Physical visible hoard.** Dungeon Keeper treasure rooms show gold physically; more efficient rooms (more surrounding room tiles/walls) store larger piles, then gems and treasures; less efficient rooms only small bags. DK2's Dungeon Heart also acts as a treasury storing up to 16,000 gold. — [Dungeon Keeper Wiki: Room Flags](https://dungeonkeeper.fandom.com/wiki/Room_Flags); [Ludo guide: DK2 room list](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/dungeon-keeper-2/a-room-list) [snippet]
- TV Tropes "Treasure Room" trope documents the visual convention of piled treasure as spectacle. — [TV Tropes: Treasure Room](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/TreasureRoom) [snippet]
- **Hold vs spend in TFT:** standard guidance is to hold at 50 for max interest and spend everything above it (XP/rolls); "rolling down" below 50 is a deliberate trade of future interest for current board strength. — [Mobalytics](https://mobalytics.gg/blog/how-to-manage-your-economy-in-teamfight-tactics-three-strategies/); [Pocket Gamer: how to econ in TFT](https://www.pocketgamer.com/teamfight-tactics/how-to-econ-in-tft-the-best-ways-to-manage-your-economy-in-teamfight-tactics/) [snippet]
- **Hold vs spend in Balatro:** interest pays only up to the cap, so money above $25 (or the voucher cap) earns nothing extra; Money Tree's unlock condition (max interest 10 rounds in a row) explicitly rewards *sitting at* the threshold. — [TrueAchievements Nest Egg](https://www.trueachievements.com/a424926/nest-egg-achievement); [games.gg](https://games.gg/balatro/guides/balatro-economy-guide/) [snippet]
- **Diminishing scaling caution:** an Escapist design piece notes damage bonuses in scaling roguelites can feel meaningless if enemy health scales too. — [Escapist: roguelite item power scale](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/roguelites-items-power-scale-game-development-vampire-survivors/) [snippet]
- **Sinks** exist mainly to keep money from becoming useless after everything is bought (Money Sink trope). — [TV Tropes: Money Sink](https://TVTropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/MoneySink) [snippet]

### Inferences (design patterns for 별의 조각, consistent with constraints)
- **Pattern A — Non-consumed aura with step tiers + soft tail.** Total held 별의 조각 sets a tier (e.g., log-scale thresholds 10 / 30 / 100 / 300 / 1,000 ...) that grants an ambient comfort effect outside factory math (e.g., monster mood/stability, weather calm on the farm, a protective "starlight" that dampens negative random events for monsters). Diminishing returns (log) mean "more is always slightly better" (no dead surplus) while early tiers deliver most of the felt value (no "should have collected more" panic). Mirrors Isaac Money=Power + Balatro caps.
- **Pattern B — Buffer sized relative to current scale (Cookie Clicker bank logic).** The pile's job is to keep some ambient bad event harmless; the "safe amount" grows slowly with farm size, so the pile stays endlessly relevant without content authoring. Risk: must not become retry insurance (rejected) — keep it continuous/passive, never consumed on trigger.
- **Pattern C — Visible pile in the world.** Render 별의 조각 holdings physically (a starlight pool/constellation in the sky or a shrine that glows brighter, Dungeon Keeper-style growing piles). Visual growth is cheap content (procedural), directly delivers "looking at the pile feels 든든", and never inflates anything.
- **Pattern D — Status/reaction.** NPCs/monsters react to tier (procedural lines/animations), echoing "holdings as reputation". Low authoring if tied to tier, not to amount.
- **Anti-patterns to avoid (from evidence):** compounding interest in the same currency (inflation; ACNH had to cut rates); holdings that increase threat (RimWorld -> players minimize wealth); holdings at risk of loss (Dark Souls/Kingdom -> anxiety, not comfort); one-time-purchase-only sinks (Money for Nothing).
- **If any spend exists**, make it a *lock-in/placement* rather than a loss (e.g., placing shards into a constellation keeps them counted toward the aura), which sidesteps loss aversion per Bottomfeeder's reframing point.

### Gaps
- No found source on games where NPCs specifically react to the player's held currency amount (status reaction); pattern D is inference only.
- No designer essay found specifically on "aura from held amount with log diminishing returns"; patterns A–D are synthesized from the cited mechanics, not documented as a named pattern.
