# Eyes-free / Haptic Game Interaction and Meaningful Manual Labor in Automation Games

Research date: 2026-11-26. Context: a controller-first game shown on an iPad or second monitor, played while the user watches anime on the main monitor.

Method note: this session's network policy blocked full-page fetches from gamedeveloper.com, gameaccessibilityguidelines.com, core.ac.uk and learn.microsoft.com. All findings below come from search-engine snippets and summaries of those pages (and others), not from reading the full documents. Exact figures should be checked against the originals before they are quoted in a final deliverable. Much of the automation and monster-game material comes from fan wikis, guides and Steam forums; it is marked as such.

## Q1. What design techniques let a player keep doing a task without looking?

### Takeaway
Eyes-free games work by moving every needed piece of information into sound or touch. Stereo or binaural position stands in for screen position, a few distinct cues stand in for UI, and the visuals are deliberately made non-essential. Accessibility guidelines frame haptics as an extra, optional, redundant channel, not the only one. For a "watch anime with your eyes" game, sound also competes with the anime audio, so haptics plus fixed, learnable input patterns are the most viable channel.

### Cited Findings
- **Xbox Accessibility Guideline (XAG) 103, "additional channels for cues":** the goal is to express key visual and audio cues through more than one sense. Example given: stereo-panned attack sounds let a blind player track both their own character and the enemy by ear. — [XAG 103, Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/103)
- **XAG 110, "haptic feedback":** haptics can serve as an extra information channel that also helps players who cannot see or hear the visual or audio cues. The guideline recommends making haptics optional and letting players adjust intensity ("Consider adding haptic cues as an optional, additional channel for important visual or audio information"). It also says "haptics should be used in addition to other ways of relaying information." — [XAG 110, Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/110)
- The XAGs are described as best practices, "not intended to act as a checklist" for compliance. The version history notes a 2.0 restructure (February 2021). — [XAG overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/guidelines); [XAG version history](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xag-version-history)
- **A Blind Legend (2015, Dowino / France Culture, iOS/Android):** headphones-only. The whole touchscreen acts as a joystick to move, swing the sword, block with the shield and chain combos. Binaural sound builds a 3D soundscape, and a guide character (the daughter, Louise) gives navigation by voice. It was crowdfunded, co-produced with France Culture (Radio France), and supported by CNC and the Rhône-Alpes Region. App Store reviews call the controls "a bit cumbersome" and complain about limited language support. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/a-blind-legend/id973483154?mt=8); [IGF entry](https://igf.com/blind-legend)
- **Blind Drive (Lo-Fi People, Tel Aviv; iOS/Android, later Steam):** an audio-only driving game "meant to be played with your eyes closed." It started as a Max/MSP sketch for an interactive installation. The visuals show only a points and lives tally, and the developers recommend playing with your eyes shut. It has 27 levels, boss fights, secret areas and a "very rude G.P.S." that carries the comedy. — [Game Developer press release](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/blind-drive-the-game-that-is-meant-to-be-played-with-your-eyes-closed); [Game Developer: Creating Blind Drive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/creating-i-blind-drive-i-an-audio-sensory-driving-game-experienced-through-sound); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/blind-drive-is-a-comedy-thriller-you-can-play-with-your-eyes-closed); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1300600)
- **1-2-Switch (Nintendo, 2017):** several minigames are built around looking at the other player rather than the screen. Ball Count asks players to tilt the Joy-Con and guess how many virtual marbles are inside it from HD Rumble alone. Game Developer argued that these haptic and audio-cue minigames could make the Switch more accessible. — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2017/02/feature_nintendo_switch_countdown_-_considering_the_joy-con); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/-i-1-2-switch-i-and-hd-rumble-can-expand-the-switch-s-accessibility) (snippet only; the full article could not be fetched)
- **The Last of Us Part II / Remastered:** DualSense haptics are used to warn the player when enemies may have spotted or heard them, and can stand in for ambient audio. The Remastered "Speech to Vibrations" option plays spoken dialogue as controller haptics, which conveys speech cadence. A blind-player setup guide calls the blind/low-vision preset "only a starting point." — [Forallwe analysis](https://www.forallwe.com/en/post/the-last-of-us-part-2-accessibility-gaming); [Blind Gaming Norway setup guide](https://blindgaming.no/en/guides/the-last-of-us-part-ii-remastered-blind-setup/); [GameFAQs thread](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/264562-playstation-5/80674204)

### Inferences
- The shared techniques are: (1) a non-spatial, whole-surface or whole-controller input (A Blind Legend's "screen is a joystick"; no aimed targets); (2) a small set of very distinct cues; (3) a voice or "guide" layer that carries narration and direction; (4) visuals reduced to a score or status the player can glance at rarely (Blind Drive).
- Applied to the anime context: audio is already taken by the anime, so the game should rely on haptics plus a fixed input rhythm or pattern, with glanceable visuals on the iPad as the backup channel. This reverses the XAG framing, where haptics are the supporting channel.
- Rhythm and fixed patterns help because the player can predict what comes next without checking, and rhythm was also the most recognizable tacton dimension (93%, see Q2).

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the Game Accessibility Guidelines full list (gameaccessibilityguidelines.com was blocked), so its exact items and their basic/intermediate/advanced levels are not quoted here.
- I found no source that studies "second-screen, peripheral-attention" play in particular, such as playing while watching video.

## Q2. How expressive is controller rumble for conveying state without vision? Research on vibrotactile pattern recognition

### Takeaway
Lab studies on "Tactons" (Brewster and Brown, University of Glasgow, mid-2000s) show that people recognize about 70% of two-parameter vibration messages. Rhythm is the most reliably recognized dimension, at about 93%. Adding a third dimension drops recognition to about 48%, and cutting the number of values brings it back to about 81%. Commercial HD rumble and DualSense can render rich textures, but reviews by blind players say directional or positional haptics are often too weak to rely on. The practical budget is a handful of distinct rhythms, possibly crossed with one coarse second dimension.

### Cited Findings
- **Tactons (Brown, Brewster and Purchase):** two-parameter Tactons encoded with rhythm and roughness reached an overall recognition rate of about 71%. Rhythm alone was recognized 93% of the time and roughness 80%. — [Glasgow eprints](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/3209); [CORE PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/1393792.pdf); [Tactons paper (typeset)](https://typeset.io/pdf/tactons-structured-tactile-messages-for-non-visual-3v7qh1p21c.pdf)
- **Multidimensional Tactons (MobileHCI 2006):** adding spatial location as a third parameter gave only 48% identification. Cutting the number of values of one parameter raised it to 81%. — [CORE PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/1393758.pdf); [Monash record](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/multidimensional-tactons-for-non-visual-information-presentation-/)
- Other related studies turned up in search but were not read: a wristband vibrotactile evaluation ([arXiv 1811.03888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.03888)), a numeric haptic encoding study ([McGill SRL, WHC 2019](https://srl.mcgill.ca/publications/2019-WHC-Numeric.pdf)), and Meta's haptic fundamentals paper ([CHI 2018](https://research.facebook.com/file/580235553133873/hapticfundamentals-chi18-cameraready-final-v4.pdf)).
- **Switch HD Rumble (Ball Count):** players report that the rolling balls feel like real objects inside the Joy-Con and that the number of balls changes the feel. Destructoid called it the only demo at the reveal event where people could feel HD Rumble directly. — [Nintendo Life forums](https://www.nintendolife.com/forums/nintendo-switch/hd_rumble_thoughts); [Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/1-2-switch-is-not-the-next-wii-sports)
- **Switch 2 (2025):** Nintendo says Joy-Con 2 HD Rumble is quieter, which makes Everybody 1-2-Switch harder to play with the new controllers. This is a reported trade-off between noise and expressiveness. — [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/switch-2-joy-cons-hd-rumble-wont-be-as-noisy-as-it-was-on-switch-nintendo-says); [Nintendo Ask the Developer Vol. 16](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/News/2025/April/Ask-the-Developer-Vol-16-Nintendo-Switch-2-Chapter-2-2787952.html)
- **DualSense / Astro's Playroom (2020):** Can I Play That called it the clearest showcase of the controller and said blind players stand to gain the most, while noting the game lacks a vibration toggle. Some effects are duplicated through touch. — [Can I Play That: PS5 impressions](https://caniplaythat.com/2020/11/06/playstation-5-accessibility-impressions/); [Astro's Playroom review](https://caniplaythat.com/2020/12/09/astros-playroom-accessibility-review/)
- **Counterpoint:** a blind reviewer of The Last of Us Part I praised the haptic dialogue but said the directional haptics were rarely strong enough to help them understand their surroundings. — [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/features/how-accessibility-options-help-disabled-gamers-beat-the-last-of-us-part-i)
- PCGamingWiki keeps a list of PC games that support DualSense haptic feedback. — [PCGamingWiki](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/List_of_games_that_support_Dualsense_haptic_feedback)

### Inferences
- **Design budget:** use rhythm as the primary code, with perhaps 3 to 5 clearly different rhythms such as "ready / done / warning / rare event". Add at most one coarse second dimension, such as intensity or left versus right motor. Avoid three-dimensional codes. These numbers are extrapolated from the Tacton results, which used lab hardware rather than game controllers.
- Textural "feel" (Ball Count) is good for *quantity or presence* (how many, is something there), which is a better fit than abstract symbols.
- Short, rhythmic "done" pulses suit a game played in the user's peripheral attention. Continuous directional haptics are unreliable according to blind players.
- Haptics need an off switch and an intensity setting (XAG 110). This matters more when the user is watching something.

### Gaps
- I found no recognition-rate study done on commercial game controllers (DualSense, Joy-Con, Xbox impulse triggers) as opposed to lab actuators.
- I found nothing on Xbox impulse triggers as a main information channel.
- Nintendo has published no technical detail on how Ball Count's patterns are generated.

## Q3. iPad / touchscreen eyes-free interaction, and controller haptics on iPad

### Takeaway
HCI research consistently recommends target-free gestures that can be made anywhere on the screen, such as swipes, multi-finger taps and flicks, for eyes-free touch input. On iPad, Apple's Game Controller and Core Haptics frameworks support haptics on game controllers, including a DualSense profile. Whether DualSense haptics fully work on iPad was disputed in 2021 coverage and needs to be tested on a device.

### Cited Findings
- **Slide Rule (Kane, Bigham and Wobbrock, ASSETS 2008, pp. 73–80):** audio-based multi-touch techniques for blind users. It uses four gesture types: a one-finger scan, a second-finger tap to select, and a multi-directional flick, among others. With 10 blind participants it was faster than a button-based screen reader but had more errors, and 7 of 10 preferred it. — [Paper PDF](https://faculty.washington.edu/wobbrock/pubs/assets-08.pdf); [Project page](https://userpages.umbc.edu/~skane/sliderule)
- **DigiTaps (UIST 2013):** eyes-free number entry that uses taps or swipes anywhere on the screen with one, two or three fingers. — [DOI](https://dx.doi.org/10.1145/2501988.2502056)
- **GestureCalc (ASSETS 2019; Chaudhuri, Perlmutter, Petelka, Garrison, Fogarty, Wobbrock and Ladner):** an eyes-free calculator whose design principle is "input gestures can be performed at any location on the screen." It cites Kane, Wobbrock and Ladner (CHI 2011, "Usable Gestures for Blind People"). — [Paper PDF](https://faculty.washington.edu/wobbrock/pubs/assets-19.01.pdf)
- **Apple Core Haptics and Game Controller:** the sample "Playing Haptics on Game Controllers" (iOS 14+) and the `GCController.haptics` property let developers create a haptic engine per controller. WWDC 2020 session 10614 says the same AHAP haptic pattern files can play on a phone or a DUALSHOCK 4. `GCDualSenseGamepad` adds adaptive triggers and motion. — [Apple docs: playing haptics on game controllers](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corehaptics/playing-haptics-on-game-controllers.md); [GCController.haptics](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/gamecontroller/gccontroller/haptics.md); [WWDC20 10614](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10614/); [GCDualSenseGamepad](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/gamecontroller/gcdualsensegamepad.md)
- **DualSense haptics on iPad (2021):** Shacknews reported that the M1 iPad Pro could use DualSense haptic feedback. The Outer Haven called this misleading, saying Apple's notes only described iOS 14.5 controller support and did not name haptics. — [Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/123939/new-m1-powered-apple-ipad-pro-can-use-ps5-dualsense-haptic-feedback); [The Outer Haven](https://www.theouterhaven.net/2021/04/apples-m1-powered-ipad-pro-supports-xbox-and-playstation-controllers-haptic-feedback)

### Inferences
- If the iPad is the input device rather than only a display, use whole-screen gestures (swipe direction, finger count, tap rhythm) and no buttons that must be aimed at.
- With a controller, AHAP files give a single authoring format for iPad and controller haptics. In practice, DualSense's richer voice-coil haptics may be reduced to basic rumble on iPad, so the design should hold up with simple rumble alone.
- iPads themselves have no Taptic Engine (an assumption not verified in this session), so haptic feedback has to come from the controller.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the current (2026) state of DualSense haptics or adaptive-trigger support on iPadOS. Test on a device.
- The full text of Kane, Wobbrock and Ladner (CHI 2011) was not found.

## Q4. In automation games, what stays manual on purpose, and why?

### Takeaway
Across the genre, automation replaces repeated *production*. The player keeps *design and layout*, *exploration and discovery* of things that unlock options (Satisfactory Hard Drives, map knowledge), and *setup and bootstrapping* (hand crafting before machines exist). The clearest example of "manual is qualitatively different" turned up outside the core factory genre: Stardew Valley's Auto-Petter gives fewer friendship points than petting by hand. I found no primary developer statement (Friday Facts, GDC talk) that justifies what is kept manual.

### Cited Findings
- **Factorio (wiki):** manual crafting ("hand crafting") is making items without assembling machines. It is "mainly required in the beginning of a game and is somewhat useful to create small amounts of items later on." It crafts sub-ingredients automatically, but all materials must be in the inventory at once, and the player cannot carry liquids, so fluid recipes are machine-only. Many materials "cannot be manually crafted and must be crafted by a machine." — [Factorio Wiki: Crafting](https://wiki.factorio.com/Crafting); [Manual crafting (ja)](https://wiki.factorio.com/Manual_crafting/ja)
- The search found no Friday Facts post on the design reasons for hand crafting. FFF #241 (new player experience) and FFF #376 (research and technology) were the closest. — [FFF #241 thread](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?p=360698); [FFF #376 thread](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?p=591450)
- **Satisfactory:** Hard Drives are found only at Crash Sites, which are located with the Object Scanner after Radio Signal research in the MAM. Each Hard Drive offers a choice between two random Alternate Recipes and can be rerolled. Counts differ by source and version: 97 Hard Drives and 85 alternates per Prima, 118 Crash Sites per the wiki. Exploration therefore produces *options* (new recipes) that a machine cannot make. — [Satisfactory Wiki: Hard Drive](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Hard_Drive); [Prima Games Hard Drives guide](https://primagames.com/gaming/complete-satisfactory-hard-drives-guide-location-map-coordinates-tier-list-and-how-to-reroll)
- **Satisfactory (community):** large builds mean more manual construction work. One player describes routing belts "several hundred meters of harrowing twists through a monster-infested cave," so exploration becomes part of logistics. Some players bounce off because of the exploration. — [Tildes thread](https://tildes.net/~games/1ix9/satisfactory_is_the_best_automation_game_ever_made_and_i_seriously_cant_recommend_it_enough) (opinion)
- **Dyson Sphere Program:** the player shapes the Dyson shell by dividing it into orbital layers and connecting nodes to choose its pattern, which is a creative or aesthetic task kept manual. — [gamefoundry.games](https://gamefoundry.games/blog/games-like-dyson-sphere-program) (listicle)
- **Shapez / Shapez 2:** the loop is designing lines and chasing better throughput, with no exploration. The only manual job is layout design. — [gamefoundry.games](https://gamefoundry.games/blog/factory-games-most-satisfying-automation) (listicle)
- **Create mod (Minecraft):** the Hand Crank gives 256 SU only while the player holds right-click, and costs hunger, so it is a stopgap. A Deployer or Mechanical Arm can automate it later. Precision Mechanisms can be made by hand using a Depot with a Deployer above it, then fully automated with a brass tunnel loop. A guide calls Create "all about design choices." — [Create GitHub wiki: Deployer](https://github.com/Creators-of-Create/Create/wiki/Deployer/d9a2f038eab78e0b2b07705396c734e244ffa7d7); [Create Fandom: Precision Mechanisms](https://create.fandom.com/wiki/Tutorials/Crafting_Precision_Mechanisms); [minecraft-modding.wiki](https://minecraft-modding.wiki/decoration/create)
- **Timberborn 1.0 (2026):** automation (sensors and conditions that turn buildings on or off) was added at 1.0 to "reduce micromanagement" and raise beaver efficiency. — [GamingOnLinux, March 2026](https://gamingonlinux.com/2026/03/timberborn-devs-announce-automation-is-coming-to-the-city-builder-in-the-1-0-release); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/city-builder/beaver-themed-city-builder-timberborn-has-gnawed-its-way-out-of-early-access-and-surprise-it-has-factorio-style-automation-now)
- **Oxygen Not Included:** the player has only indirect control of duplicants. Automation means logic gates and sensors (gas pressure, temperature) driving pumps and other actuators. — [nishio scrapbox](https://scrapbox.io/nishio-en/Oxygen_Not_Included); [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/OxygenNotIncluded)
- **Palworld (fan guides):** Pals assigned to a base automatically look for tasks that match their work suitabilities. Sanity (SAN) drains in proportion to work done, and faster traits drain it faster. A Pal goes idle when its job finishes unless it has another suitability. Players keep up sanity with beds, hot springs and good food. One guide says the 1.0 release raised the work-suitability cap from 4 to 10, which conflicts with older guides that say 1–4. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1623730/discussions/0/592936492464264816/); [hostedgg guide](https://hostedgg.com/blog/palworld-work-suitability-base-pals-guide); [palworld1.wiki](https://palworld1.wiki/base/palworld-10-base-design); [dungeonpath guide](https://dungeonpath.com/posts/palworld/pal-management-optimization-guide/)
- **Stardew Valley Auto-Petter:** pets every animal in its Barn or Coop each day and gives about the same mood boost as petting by hand, but only **8 friendship points versus 15** for manual petting. Manual petting stacks on top. Its main job is to prevent friendship and mood loss. It is expensive (50,000g at Joja after the Community Development form, or a rare drop from mine containers in certain states). — [Stardew Wiki: Auto-Petter](https://stardew.wiki/auto-petter/); [Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/stardew-valley/auto-petter/)

### Inferences
- **What stays manual across these games:**
  1. Bootstrapping, before machines exist (Factorio hand crafting, the Create Hand Crank).
  2. Layout and design, which is the real game in Shapez, DSP and Create.
  3. Discovery that unlocks options, not output (Satisfactory Hard Drives).
  4. Creative shaping (DSP sphere patterns).
  5. Upkeep of living workers (Palworld sanity, food and beds).
- **"Manual is qualitatively better":** Stardew's Auto-Petter is the cleanest pattern. Automation sets a *floor* (no decay), and hand interaction adds a *bonus* on top (15 points versus 8, stacking). This keeps manual play meaningful without making it required.
- Satisfactory's pattern is that manual exploration yields *choice*, not more stuff. A machine can make 1,000 screws, but only you can find a new recipe.
- For a hand-play game watched alongside anime, the manual action should be a low-attention, rhythmic task that is still the *best* source of some resource, such as bond points or rare drops, while automation keeps the base running.

### Gaps
- I found no primary developer source (Friday Facts, GDC talk, dev blog) that explains *why* certain tasks are kept manual. The reasons above are inferred from mechanics.
- I found no details on Techtonica's manual tasks, Satisfactory's Somersloops or Mercer Spheres, or Factorio Space Age changes to hand crafting.
- Create's designer (simibubi) did not appear in any design-philosophy source.

## Q5. Monster-collection and farm games: repetitive bonding that scales without becoming overpowered

### Takeaway
The reliable patterns are capped or decaying bond meters (Stardew), bonds that unlock new *capabilities* rather than raw stats (Cassette Beasts fusion), and worker upkeep that limits how much labor scales (Palworld sanity). Detailed sources on Ooblets, Temtem and Slime Rancher bonding were thin.

### Cited Findings
- **Stardew Valley:** friendship and mood are lost if animals are not petted. The Auto-Petter prevents that loss but gives fewer points (8 versus 15), so hand care is still the best way to raise friendship. — [Stardew Wiki](https://stardew.wiki/auto-petter/); [Stardew forums on friendship decay](https://forums.stardewvalley.net/threads/do-friendship-points-decrease-when-you-dont-pet-animals-like-sheep-chicken.31517/)
- **Cassette Beasts (Bytten Studio, 2023):** the bond with a human companion directly affects Fusion, a core mechanic, and a higher bond strengthens the fused form. Without a partner you cannot fuse at all. Modular monster parts allow about 14,000 automatically generated fusions. — [Magic Game World](https://magicgameworld.com/?p=79594); [Wireframe](https://wireframe.raspberrypi.com/articles/creature-collecting-gets-an-analogue-upgrade-in-cassette-beasts); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Cassette_Beasts)
- **Palworld:** work output is limited by sanity, which drains faster for faster workers. Lazy traits such as Slacker (−30% work speed) can be worse than leaving the slot empty. This makes the roster a care-and-selection problem rather than "more Pals = more output." — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1623730/discussions/0/592936492464264816/); [dungeonpath guide](https://dungeonpath.com/posts/palworld/pal-management-optimization-guide/) (fan guides)
- **Slime Rancher 2:** Quantum Drones arrived around the 1.0 / 1.1.0 updates (sources disagree; one says patch 1.1.0, December 2025). They move food from silos and gardens to Auto-Feeders and collect plorts from corrals. Auto-Feeders let players set food and feeding intervals. — [switchbladegaming guide](https://www.switchbladegaming.com/cozy-games/slime-rancher-2-quantum-drone-guide/); [Sportskeeda: Auto-Feeder](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/how-use-auto-feeder-slime-rancher-2) (SEO guides; unverified)
- **Ooblets:** described only generally, as a cozy farming game with dance battles where you collect ooblets and hold dance parties. — [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/gaming/these-indie-monster-tamers-are-ideal-for-those-tired-of-pokemon-and-palworld)

### Inferences
- Bond designs that scale without becoming overpowered: (a) bond gates a *qualitative* unlock (a fusion, skill or cosmetic) and stops there; (b) bond decays slowly and automation only holds it at the current level; (c) a creature's labor costs upkeep (sanity) that hand care restores, so hand interaction becomes the "refuel" action.
- For the anime use case, "petting" or "feeding" fits well as an eyes-free rhythmic action (a few button presses, each confirmed by a distinct haptic pulse). It can repeat forever because its value is capped per creature per day, as in Stardew where each animal can be petted once a day.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable sources on Ooblets friendship mechanics, Temtem breeding or bonding, or exactly what Slime Rancher 2 drones cannot do.
- I found no developer interviews explaining how these games balance bond scaling.
