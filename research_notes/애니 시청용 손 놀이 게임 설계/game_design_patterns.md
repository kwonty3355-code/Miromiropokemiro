> Context for the report writer: the target game is a monster-farm + Minecraft-Create-style factory game. The "anime mode" action must be non-automatable, not affect factory math, endless, eyes-free, no fail state, no decisions, no sound, no nagging, controller-based, and must give an accumulating benefit to the base without infinite economy bonuses.

## 1. Genres and concrete examples: what mechanics make them work in the background?

### Takeaway
Since 2024, "screen-edge / desktop companion" idlers (Rusty's Retirement, Ropuka's Idle Island, Bongo Cat, Tiny Pasture, Cornerpond) have become a recognized Steam micro-genre. They work in the background because they (a) take up only part of the screen, (b) keep progressing with almost no input, (c) have no fail state, and (d) put the reward in visible accumulation (a growing farm, a counter, cosmetics) instead of in decisions. "Podcast games" (PowerWash Simulator, mowing sims, Vampire Survivors, farming sims) work through a different mechanism: a repetitive motor loop with instant feedback and low cognitive load, which leaves the verbal and auditory channels free.

**Idle/incremental (academic framing)**
- The CHI 2018 study "Playing to Wait: A Taxonomy of Idle Games" (Alharthi, Alsaedi, Toups Dugas, Tanenbaum, Hammer) analyzed 66 idle games with grounded theory, plus 10 non-idle games as a boundary check. It defines idle games as a minimalist genre where the game keeps running with little interaction and that is "primarily played by not playing". ### Inferences
- **[Inference]** There are two distinct mechanisms. (A) *Idle/screen-edge*: progress happens while the player ignores the game, and the reward is glancing at it. (B) *Podcast/motor-loop*: the player is actively doing a rhythmic, low-cognition motor task, and the reward is instant per-action feedback. The user's "anime mode" (non-automatable, controller, eyes-free) is type B, not type A. Type-A games should be mined for *reward presentation* (counters, accumulation, cosmetics). Type-B games should be mined for *the action itself* (PowerWash's single satisfying verb, Vampire Survivors' one-stick input, mowing's steady rhythm).
- **[Inference]** Bongo Cat is the closest structural analogue to the requested anime mode. The player's input happens *for another purpose*, the game only counts it, rewards come on a time/activity cadence, and the rewards are purely cosmetic and collectible. That keeps it endless without touching any economy. The 10-to-1 trade-up turns infinite duplicates into a sink.
- **[Inference]** The Ropuka "142 hours and I haven't done anything" complaint and the Chillquarium "not engaging enough" complaint suggest a failure mode: endless accumulation with no *visible* change in the world reads as nothing. Accumulation needs a visible, persistent artifact.
- **[Inference]** Tiny Pasture's complaints (punishing gacha, confusing breeding/health) show that adding management or fail-like states to a background game generates friction. This supports the "no decisions / no fail" constraint.

## 2. Design principles articulated by designers, GDC talks and research (low-attention, ambient, prestige, soft caps)

### Takeaway
The best-documented formal principles come from idle-game math (Pecorella/Kongregate: exponential costs, prestige with flat sublinear prestige formulas, nested prestige) and from HCI work on idle games and second-screen attention. Explicit essays on "low-attention design" for non-idle games are sparse; most of that wisdom is in list articles and reviews.

### Cited Findings
- Anthony Pecorella (Kongregate; producer of AdVenture Capitalist) gave the GDC talks "Idle Games: The Mechanics and Monetization of Self-Playing Games" (GDC 2015), "Idle Chatter: What We Can Learn From Self-Playing Games" (GDC 2016) and "Quest for Progress: The Math and Design of Idle Games" (GDC Europe 2016). He also wrote a three-part "Math of Idle Games" series on Game Developer and published spreadsheets at kon.gg/idle-math-spreadsheets. — [GDC Vault Quest for Progress](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023876/Quest-for-Progress-The-Math); [GDC Vault Idle Chatter](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023208/Idle-Chatter-What-We-Can); [GDC Vault 2015](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022066/Idle-Games-The-Mechanics-and); [Kongregate blog](https://blog.kongregate.com/quest-for-progress-the-math-of-idle-games)
- Pecorella defines prestige as resetting most of the game (especially generators and multipliers) in exchange for a prestige currency and/or persistent multipliers that accelerate the next run. Prestige can be nested ("meta prestige"). "Idle Chatter" lists "prestige variation" (rules change on prestige) as a 2015 innovation. — [SlideShare Quest for Progress](https://de.slideshare.net/AnthonyPecorella/quest-for-progress-gdc-europe-2016); [SlideShare Idle Chatter](https://pt.slideshare.net/AnthonyPecorella/idle-chatter-gdc-2016-59734260)
- Math of Idle Games Part III: prestige formulas based on lifetime earnings have very flat curves, so pushing further in a run yields rapidly diminishing returns. This is the deliberate tool that makes prestige timing a soft decision. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-math-of-idle-games-part-iii) (via search summary)
- Cookie Clicker: ascending converts cookies baked into prestige levels and heavenly chips at 1:1. Heavenly upgrades unlock in tiers revealing 5% to 100% of prestige potential, costing from 11 to about 1.1 billion chips. There is no hard prestige cap. — [Cookie Clicker Wiki](https://cookieclicker.wiki.gg/wiki/Prestige); [PocketGamer guide](https://www.pocketgamer.com/cookie-clicker/heavenly-chips-guide)
- HCI on second screens: in a lab study, highly interactive TV-companion games interleaved with content drew up to 70% of attention onto the tablet game. The authors say it is "still unclear which level of interactivity and synchronization leads to which level of attention". — [NSF PAR paper](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10174274)
- Emmerich, Liszio and Masuch (ACM) propose design patterns for second-screen gaming and argue for comprehensive guidelines that make the second screen core to the game. — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/16115421/Defining_Second_Screen_Gaming_Exploration_of_New_Design_Patterns); [DOI](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F2663806.2663855)
- Marcus Carter ("Screen ecologies, multi-gaming and designing for different registers of engagement", Game Developer) argues some play styles do not require full attention and so encourage multiple screens. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/screen-ecologies-multi-gaming-and-designing-for-different-registers-of-engagement)
- Mark Eyles (ambient games): "an ambient game would not be ambient if playing it stopped when you moved your attention away from it." — [inkl / interview](https://www.inkl.com/news/how-to-game-without-knowing-it-mark-eyles)
- Game Developer has an essay titled "The rise of games you mostly don't play" (title only; content not retrieved). — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-rise-of-games-you-mostly-don-t-play)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** The 70%-attention finding warns that interactivity that is *synchronized with* or *interleaved into* the show steals attention. Anime mode should be *asynchronous* with the anime: no prompts timed to anything, no events requiring reaction.
- **[Inference]** Prestige and lifetime-earnings formulas are designed to manage *infinite economy growth*, which the user explicitly wants to avoid. The transferable idea is the *shape*: sublinear (e.g., sqrt or log) conversion of endless effort into a bounded-rate benefit. Applied to non-economic outputs (cosmetics, history, visual density), it avoids balance issues entirely.
- **[Inference]** A useful framing for the user: "power axis capped or absent, presentation axis unbounded". Let the infinite tail live on axes that cannot break factory math, such as visual decoration, monster affection/appearance, chronicle entries and collection counts.
- **[Inference]** Rusty's Retirement's Focus Mode, which deliberately *slows* production while the player is attentive elsewhere, shows that designers will trade efficiency for unobtrusiveness. That is a precedent for anime mode giving a *small, slow* benefit.

- Could not access the GDC Vault videos or the full Game Developer article text. Pecorella's exact recommended prestige exponents (commonly cited as square/cube-root) are not verified here.
- Found no dedicated GDC talk on "low-attention design" or "podcast games" as a design discipline. The literature is fragmented across HCI papers and journalism.

## 3. "Check when I want, see what I did, feel good" without nagging: offline summaries, recaps, counters, logs

### Takeaway
The dominant patterns are: (1) a persistent always-visible counter (Bongo Cat); (2) capped offline progress computed on return (Melvor Idle, Clicker Heroes); (3) the world itself as the summary (Rusty's farm, Ropuka's island); and (4) reward cadence tied to elapsed time or activity, delivered as an object the player can open at leisure (Bongo Cat chest). Sourced evidence on "no notifications" as an explicit design goal is thin.

### Cited Findings
- Bongo Cat shows a counter beneath the cat that increments with every key or click. Clicking the counter shows time until the next drop. Drops arrive as chests the player opens. — [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/what-is-bongo-cat/); [bo3.gg](https://bo3.gg/articles/how-to-get-hats-in-bongo-cat)
- Melvor Idle advertises offline progression. A Steam community post states offline calculation is capped at 24 hours in the base game; another guide says 18 hours (possibly outdated). — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1267910/Melvor_Idle); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/591762563949134719); conflicting: [ItemLevel](https://itemlevel.net/is-melvor-idle-online-only/)
- Clicker Heroes' developer blog has a post on its offline progression design (content not retrieved). — [Clicker Heroes blog](https://blog.clickerheroes.com/offline-progression-in-clicker-heroes/embed/)
- Ambient design principle: the game continues when attention moves away (Eyles). — [inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/how-to-game-without-knowing-it-mark-eyles)
- Rusty's Retirement's design intent is "forget it for an hour" at the screen bottom; the farm visibly grows. — [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/cozy-corner-stay-productive-with-this-farming-sim-that-uses-a-fraction-of-your-screen/)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** For anime mode, the recap should be *pull, not push*. Show a quiet "this session" card (e.g., "24 min · 1,820 strokes · 3 new chronicle pages · Monster X affection +1 heart") *only when the player exits the mode*. Keep a lifetime counter somewhere browsable. Never use toasts or sounds during the mode (the user requires no sound/nagging).
- **[Inference]** A good recap uses multiple scales: one raw counter that always goes up (strokes/minutes), plus discrete "milestone objects" (pages, decorations, stamps). Raw counters satisfy "I did this much", and discrete objects give memorability. Bongo Cat combines exactly these (counter + chest).
- **[Inference]** Cap offline or idle accrual, as Melvor's 24h cap does. Since anime mode is active, not idle, the analogous rule is a *daily or rolling soft cap with diminishing returns per session* (e.g., full value for the first ~30-60 minutes, then a slower tail that never hits zero). The player always "gets something" but can't farm economic advantage.
- **[Inference]** "The world is the summary": have accumulation physically appear in the base (a growing mural, a monster shrine filling with offerings, a garden of decorative blocks). Returning to the base then *is* the recap, with no UI pop-up needed.

### Gaps
- No primary source found describing Melvor's or any desktop idler's "welcome back" screen UX in detail, or any designer explicitly arguing against push notifications. Mobile-idle notification practice was not researched.

## 4. Infinite meaningful sinks that don't break balance

### Takeaway
Proven patterns include finite-but-huge collection logs with rank tiers (OSRS: 1,925 slots), museum donation with milestone rewards and a capstone (Stardew: 95 items, Stardrop at completion), cosmetic gacha with duplicate trade-up (Bongo Cat 10-to-1), decoration spending (Ropuka, Rusty), and stamp collection (Ropuka). True infinity is usually achieved by layering: a finite collection, then rarer tiers or variants, then pure counters or decorative accumulation with no cap.

### Cited Findings
- OSRS Collection Log: 1,925 slots tracking 1,717 unique items. Ranks are earned by slot counts (top listed: Gilded at 1,525, about 90% of total) and claimed as a staff from The Collector. Duplicates are tracked only up to item-specific caps. — [OSRS Wiki](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Collection_log)
- Stardew Valley Museum: 95 donations (42 artifacts + 53 minerals). Milestone rewards come along the way (e.g., Rusty Key at 60 donations; "Treasure Trove" achievement at 40). Completion grants a Stardrop (permanent +max energy) and "A Complete Collection". — [Stardew Wiki](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Museum); [Wonderful Museums guide](https://www.wonderfulmuseums.com/museum/stardew-museum-rewards/)
- Bongo Cat: endless cosmetic drops on a cadence. The 10-same-rarity to 1-higher-rarity exchange converts infinite duplicates into progress toward rare items; the legendary rate is 1/500,000. — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3419430/Bongo_Cat/); [GamesFuze](https://gamesfuze.com/guides/bongo-cat-complete-hat-item-guide-tiers-exchange-legendaries/)
- Ropuka: grass currency goes to tools (power), grass quality (power), or island decoration (cosmetic sink), plus stamps (collection). — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/meet-a-frog-companion-who-loves-cutting-grass-collect-stamps-in-this-casual-game); [Comfy Cozy Gaming](https://www.comfycozygaming.com/2025/02/08/review-ropukas-idle-island)
- Rusty's Retirement: player choices are seed planting plus placement of facilities and decorations, so decoration is a core sink. — [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/cozy-corner-stay-productive-with-this-farming-sim-that-uses-a-fraction-of-your-screen/)
- Cookie Clicker's heavenly upgrades are tier-gated with costs spanning 11 to about 1.1B chips, an extreme example of a long-tail sink. — [PocketGamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/cookie-clicker/heavenly-chips-guide)

### Inferences (pattern catalogue applied to the user's constraints)
- **[Inference] Tiered collection with a capstone, then an infinite tail.** A finite "anime-mode codex" (e.g., 100 monster sketches or poses) with milestone rewards at 10/25/50%. Then infinite "variants" (colors, seasonal frames) at decreasing rates. Then a pure lifetime counter. This mirrors OSRS ranks + Stardew milestones + Bongo trade-up.
- **[Inference] Mastery tracks with diminishing returns on power, unlimited on prestige-of-display.** For example, a "bond" level per monster where levels 1-10 give small capped QoL or comfort effects (cosmetic aura, idle animation, nameplate star), and 10+ only adds visual stars or titles. No factory multiplier.
- **[Inference] Decorative accumulation in the base.** Each session "carves" or "weaves" one decorative block, a mural tile, a bonsai growth stage or a quilt square that is placed automatically (no decision) somewhere in the base. Supply is effectively infinite, value is purely aesthetic, and it makes the base visibly "lived-in" over hundreds of hours.
- **[Inference] World restoration / territory reveal.** Anime-mode strokes slowly restore a cosmetic region (e.g., heal withered scenery around the farm, unfog a decorative map). Restoration is finite per region but regions can be procedurally generated or cycled seasonally.
- **[Inference] Chronicle / history log.** Auto-written diary entries ("Day 212: Mochi and Pip watched the rain together") generated from session time and base state. The log is infinite, it costs no balance, and it doubles as the recap.
- **[Inference] Population or ecosystem growth with soft caps.** Ambient non-working creatures (butterflies, fireflies, wild critters) grow in number with effort, using a log-curve visual density that saturates gently. Performance budget becomes the natural hard cap.
- **[Inference] Trade-up / conversion sinks** (Bongo Cat style) let infinite duplicates remain meaningful without new content. Any duplicate output of anime mode should be convertible upward.
- **[Inference] Where a gameplay benefit is wanted,** make it *non-stacking or rate-limited*. Examples: a once-per-day "well-rested" buff to monster mood that does not compound; one-time unlocks; or benefits to *player convenience* (cosmetic slots, storage for decorations) rather than *throughput*. That satisfies "accumulating benefit without infinite economy bonuses".

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence on museum/log systems specifically designed for *infinite* tails; the OSRS and Stardew systems are finite. Infinite-tail claims are design inference.
- No source found quantifying player satisfaction for cosmetic vs. power progression in idle games.

## 5. Controller-based repetitive rhythms (non-clicking) as the core loop

### Takeaway
Sourced examples of a controller-driven rhythmic core loop that is designed for half-attention are: Vampire Survivors (one stick, auto-attack), PowerWash Simulator (a sustained trigger plus slow sweeping aim, instant feedback, no wrong method), and mowing sims (steady sweeping passes, no penalties or score). Bongo Cat proves the reward side: arbitrary rhythmic input counted and converted into cosmetic drops. No sourced example was found of a game whose sole loop is *eyes-free* controller rhythm.

### Cited Findings
- Vampire Survivors: left stick only, attacks automatic at intervals; "No Simultaneous Buttons"; reviewers call it extremely approachable for casual players. A disabled reviewer found it easier than twin-stick shooters but noted friction when shifting the hand between buttons and stick. — [Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/review/vampire-survivors/single-stick-masterpiece); [Family Gaming Database](https://www.familygamingdatabase.com/accessibility/Vampire+Survivors); [Equal Entry](https://equalentry.com/?p=174954)
- PowerWash Simulator: core design is keeping everything out of the way of a single satisfying verb, with instant and impactful feedback and no wrong method. — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/powerwash-sim-devs-on-making-cleaning-fun-advanced-cro-1847319502)
- Lawn Mowing Simulator: "long stretches of rote repetition"; critics describe a zen-like state from repetitive tasks. — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/1847496842); [Yahoo/Guardian](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/impossible-play-more-30-minutes-090014376.html)
- It's Literally Just Mowing: "no penalties, no score... the perfect casual zen experience." — [App Store](https://apple.co/3fpZZlu)
- Bongo Cat: every input counts with no correctness judgment, and the cat animates per input. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/life-sim/one-of-the-biggest-games-on-steam-right-now-is-bongo-cat-a-cat-with-a-hat-who-smacks-your-windows-taskbar-like-a-bongo-drum-when-you-type)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** Eyes-free controller loop recipe drawn from the above:
  - (1) *No accuracy judgment*: every input counts, as in Bongo Cat and mowing.
  - (2) *Continuous analog input* (stick circles, trigger squeezes, gyro rocking) rather than discrete timing, so there is no rhythm game to fail.
  - (3) *Haptic feedback instead of visual or sound* (rumble ticks per "stroke", a stronger pulse on milestone). This fits the no-sound, eyes-free requirement. Haptics are the only feedback channel that doesn't compete with anime audio or video.
  - (4) *Single verb*, as in PowerWash.
  - (5) *Rate-limit value per time, not per input*, so mashing faster doesn't help. This also prevents macro or turbo-controller automation from yielding more.
- **[Inference]** Non-automatability: tie value to *varied human-like analog input over time* (e.g., require stick motion that changes direction, or tie it to the controller being held via gyro micro-motion). Cap per-minute value so a turbo button or macro earns no more than a relaxed human does. This makes automation pointless rather than impossible.
- **[Inference]** Candidate verbs that fit a monster farm: brushing or grooming a monster (stick circles), kneading or knitting (alternating triggers), rocking a cradle or hammock (gyro tilt), petting (touchpad strokes), polishing a gem or tool (stick circles). Each maps to an auto-placed cosmetic output (fur shine, knitted quilt squares, polished trophies).

### Gaps
- No sourced example found of a commercial game explicitly marketed as "eyes-free" or "play while watching TV with a controller".
- No research found on haptic-only feedback loops for relaxation games. That rationale is inference.
- Euro Truck Simulator, Minecraft strip-mining and Stardew farming as podcast activities appear in list articles, but no designer commentary on their rhythm or attention mechanics was retrieved.
